# Corpus — facing editions

One folder per edition, `corpus/<slug>/`: `edition.yaml` (the header: title, languages, where the texts come from and how far they have been checked, plus the `# ` heading and the `Conventions:` paragraph), `original.md` and `en.md` (one language each, `[p. N]` at every printed page start; headings, `⁂` and standalone page marks stand in both; an original-only text has `original.md` only), and, when they exist, `glossary.md` and `notes.md`. `tools/reader.py` merges the two languages into the facing text (original in quoted blocks, English in plain paragraphs) and writes it to the site as `facing/<name>.md`; those generated facing files, downloadable from the site, are what the Claude Project takes, not `corpus/*.md`. A new edition that arrives as one interleaved file goes through `python3 tools/split.py corpus/<name>.md` (`export_all.sh` stages its output in `staging/` and runs `tools/split.py --into corpus` on it, which skips every edition that already has a folder); `reader.py` also reads such a file as it is, which is how the two Trendelenburg *Logische Untersuchungen* originals (`trendelenburg-lu-1-original.md`, `trendelenburg-lu-2-original.md`) are still kept: their footnotes are quoted blocks, which a folder cannot hold. All translations are machine-made (Claude) and reviewed against the original by Claude, not by a human translator, unless the header says otherwise. The folder is the source: corrections go into `corpus/<slug>/` (`original.md`, `en.md`, `edition.yaml`); nothing regenerates it, and `export_all.sh` only adds editions that have no folder yet.

Paragraph standard (editions whose `edition.yaml` says `paragraphs: printed`; the others are paired page by page): `original.md` holds the printed paragraphs, never cut at a page turn, `[p. N]` inline where the page begins; `en.md` has exactly one unit (paragraph or heading) per original unit, same order, `[p. N]` at the matching point; so paragraph n of one file pairs with paragraph n of the other. The reader cuts each pair at its shared `[p. N]` marks for display, one card per printed page.

## What an edition holds

Every edition has the same reading key (its `edition.yaml` repeats it in `conventions:`), but the texts come in four shapes; the table's *layout* and *original text* columns say which:

1. **Original and English, paired.** Fifteen editions made from the group's page files, plus Klages, *Der Geist als Widersacher der Seele*, and the Trendelenburg chapters. In the photocopy editions the original is the OCR of the scan, uncorrected and cut where the English paragraphs begin: the scan in the PDF edition is the authority. In the clean-text editions (Boutroux, Brentano, Exner, Klages *Geist*, Trendelenburg) it is a corrected transcription. Bardili follows the paragraph standard below.
2. **Original page and English, paired by page.** Luz y Caballero, Rubio, Vaz Ferreira: the original is the OCR of a whole printed page, not cut at paragraphs, beside that page's English.
3. **English only.** Bachofen, Bello, Clavigero I–IV, Gamarra (both), Hostos, Klages *Errungenschaften*, Palágyi (both), Tiberghien, Wolff: the original is in the scan of the PDF edition only.
4. **Original only, no translation.** The 37 Vasconcelos texts, Dacqué, Obereit, Klages *Persönlichkeit* and the two Trendelenburg volumes.

Page marks, in every file:
- `[p. N]` — a printed page begins here, numbered as printed (Roman for front matter). Cite these.
- `[p. II.N]` — page N of vol. II, where the volumes are paginated separately (Exner, Fries).
- `[scan N]`, `[PDF N]` — a page of the scan or PDF that carries no printed number (title pages and plates; Henríquez Ureña throughout, Hostos and Bachofen in part, because their recovery found no printed number). Cite them as "scan N" / "PDF p. N".

Originals keep the print's spelling, long s (`ſ`) included: search both `ſ` and `s`. The English is machine-made and reviewed against the original by Claude, not by a human translator; the *translation* column marks the one edition that was not reviewed (Klages, *Geist*). Fifteen editions have a `glossary.md` with the translation's terminology; the others have none yet.

`tools/check_corpus.py` prints the consistency checks of the 24 October audit (`AUDIT-2026-10-24.md`) for the facing files: `python3 tools/reader.py corpus /tmp/site && python3 tools/check_corpus.py /tmp/site/facing`.
