## General remark — Provenance of the edition
Source: the Revue Bleue offprint of 1902 (Google scan, 50 pages); clean-text edition, retyped following the print's own page breaks. — prepared from the production records, Oct 2026

## General remark — Editorial policies
Obvious misprints that yield a non-word were silently corrected in the French (for instance "nons" to "nous", "dn" to "du", "homines" to "hommes"); misprints that are real words or merely ungrammatical are kept as printed and noted below. The title-page line "A L'INSTITUT" is printed with an unaccented capital A and is kept so. Quotations are given complete in both columns; foreign-language quotations (Goethe, Faust, Latin, Greek) are kept in the original with the English in square brackets. "Conscience" is rendered "consciousness" throughout, since no moral sense is in play; *l'esprit* is "spirit" where it is contrasted with the flesh or is the source of personality (pp. 12, 13, 22) and "mind" where it is the mystic's mind reviewing its life (p. 10). Letter-spacing in the print is justification only, not emphasis. — prepared from the production records, Oct 2026

## [p. 8] Misprint and spacing
The French keeps "le point départ" (for "le point *de* départ"); the English reads "point of departure", as on p. 7. "s'avilit ;" is set with the same thin space before the semicolon as the rest of the page. — prepared from the production records, Oct 2026

## [p. 9] Greek terms, and a grammatical misprint
The small, blurred Greek is read as κάθαρσις and ἄσκησις in standard accentuation (an OCR reading suggested odd accents that the scan does not support); they are glossed [purification] and [ascetic exercise]. "tout la force" (for "toute") is kept in French and rendered "the whole force". — prepared from the production records, Oct 2026

## [p. 11] Missing comma; Goethe
In "s'y unir pleinement à s'y savoir" no comma is printed after "pleinement"; the French is kept as printed, while the English inserts the comma of the series. Goethe's German prose sentence is kept inline in italics with an English gloss in brackets. — prepared from the production records, Oct 2026

## [p. 18] Folio misprint (the thirteenth page of the offprint)
The folio of this page is printed as "18" (blurred, but clearly an 18 on inspection), although the page falls between pp. 12 and 14; the misprint is kept as printed, so there are two pages numbered 18 in this edition. The paragraph that continues from p. 12 is carried over unchanged. A German couplet (Goethe) is set as a verse block followed by its bracketed English rendering. The Latin "Omnia sana sanis" is glossed [All things are sound to the sound]; *l'esprit* in "to spirit" (as the source of personality) is rendered "spirit". — prepared from the production records, Oct 2026

## [p. 14] Section II; "being" and "doing"
The numeral II of the second section falls inside a line, after "Il y a des fautes / cachées": the sentence is split across the break and the English follows it ("There are faults" / "concealed, which…"). "Le faire n'est que le phénomène de l'être" is rendered "doing is only the phenomenon of being" (not italic in print). The echo of Hamlet ("more things … dreamt of in our philosophy") is given in the familiar English wording. — prepared from the production records, Oct 2026

## [p. 15] "Fonds" (pp. 15–16)
"Fonds" (in "fonds véritable" and "fonds de spontanéité") is rendered "ground" throughout. On p. 16 "aux yeux dn mystique" is corrected to "du", and the Latin is glossed [God is understood only so far as he is loved.] — prepared from the production records, Oct 2026

## [p. 18] Faust (the page properly numbered 18)
The Faust line is kept as printed, "hoff' ich … finden :", as a single italic verse line, with the gloss [In your Nothing I hope to find the All:]. Boutroux's French rendering that follows is a separate paragraph. The sentence before it ends with a full stop in the print; the English uses a colon. — prepared from the production records, Oct 2026
