## General remark — Provenance of the edition
Source: Mainz: Kirchheim 1867, HathiTrust/Google scan of the Harvard copy (1-bit, 600 dpi). The scan contains the front matter three times; the complete set was used (title, dedication, Vorwort V–VI, Inhalt VII–VIII). The notes are numbered continuously through the main text and restart in the Beilage (p. 234). The Greek (Aristotle) and Latin (Avicenna, Aquinas) were retyped from the page images, because the scan's OCR garbles Greek. — prepared from the production records, Oct 2026

## General remark — Editorial policies
Footnote cues keep the numbers printed in the book and are never renumbered; where the print itself is faulty the German column reproduces it and the English column gives the sense (see the individual notes). The errata list on p. 252 has been applied in the English column only; the German column keeps the printed reading. Greek and Latin quotations are given as printed in both columns and are not translated, except for German glosses inside them. Evident non-words in Greek or Latin (broken accents, breathings, iota subscripts, misread letters) are silently set to the standard form in both columns, whereas real words or variants are kept as printed; where the scan was unclear the standard form was chosen. Emphasis follows the letter-spacing or italics of the print, judged from the page image rather than from the OCR. Titles of works stay in the language printed (*De Anim.*, *Metaph.*, Brandis's *Geschichte der griechisch-römischen Philosophie*); Aristotle's works named in German prose are translated. Names are modernised in the English (Ibn Rushd, Renan, Shahrastani, Dicaearchus, Thomas Aquinas, Suárez, Descartes for "Cartesius"). Abbreviations in references: "u. a. a. O." (*und an anderen Orten*) is "and elsewhere", "a. a. O." and "a. g. O." are "loc. cit.", "S." meaning *siehe* is "See", "Anm." and "A." are "n."; where "n." refers to one of Brentano's numbered paragraphs (e.g. "Theil III. n. 3") it is rendered "no." to avoid confusion with "note". Footnotes printed run together on one line and separated by a dash are set as separate notes. Where the German breaks a page in the middle of a word or clause, the English is divided at the nearest point that its own word order allows, so a sentence may begin or end a little differently across a page break; the seams in question were checked, and the more awkward ones are noted below. Pages that open a Part or the Appendix (pp. 74, 79 and 234, which have no page marker in the text) print no folio.
— prepared from the production records, Oct 2026

## General remark — Recurring terms
Beyond the book's glossary, the following renderings are used consistently. *Leiden / leidend* is "being affected" or "undergoing" (the *leidensfähiger* Verstand of Averroes is the "passible intellect"); *Energie* is "actuality"; *Wirkung* is "effect", *das Wirkende* "the agent", *wirkendes Princip* "active principle" in the discussion of the active intellect but "efficient principle" or "efficient cause" where it is opposed to the final cause (*Zweckursache*, "final cause"; *Zweck* "end"), notably in the Appendix; *Gefühlssinn* is "sense of touch"; *Zornmuth* "anger"; *Begierde* "desire"; *Habitus* "habit" or "habitus"; *Gottheit* "deity" (neuter) and *Gott* "God" (masculine). *Vorstellung* in its ordinary, non-technical sense is "notion", not the technical "presentation".
