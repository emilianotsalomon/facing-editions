## General remark — Provenance of the edition
Source: Berlin: Akademie 1905, HathiTrust/Google scan of the Harvard copy, 225 pages; the Anmerkungen concordance and the Inhalt are kept. Hegel's quotations are translated afresh with Hegel-standard terms (not Knox); the abbreviations of the Anmerkungen are translated (S./Z./v. o. → p./l./from top). The print's sheet lines ("Philos.-histor. Abh. 1905. IV. N", every 8 pages) are normalised. The German in this file is OCR of the scan, left uncorrected; where it and the scan differ, the scan in the PDF edition is the authority. — prepared from the production records, Oct 2026

## General remark — Editorial policies
Quotations from Hegel's manuscripts and letters, and from Kant, Schiller, Fichte, Schelling, Schleiermacher and the Bible, are translated afresh from the German as Dilthey prints them, using the standard Hegel terms (no reliance on Knox or other published English versions); Dilthey's cuts (… and —) are kept. Hegel's *aufheben* / *Aufhebung* is "sublate" / "sublation" where it is Hegel's technical term, and "cancel" or "abolish" where Dilthey uses it loosely (e.g. pp. 80, 87). Book titles are set in roman, as in the print; Greek, Latin and French phrases are kept as printed and glossed in square brackets only where needed. Names are modernised (Kato → Cato, Kadmus → Cadmus, Deukalion → Deucalion, Eckardt → Eckhart, Empedokles → Empedocles). Sperrdruck (letter-spacing), the print's only emphasis, is rendered as emphasis; apparently bold lines in the scan are uneven inking, not bold type. A subjunctive of reported speech ("he wrote", "he had once …") is signalled in English where the German has the *Konjunktiv* (e.g. pp. 19, 40, 86). In the Anmerkungen the abbreviations are translated (S. → p., Z. → l., v. o. → from top). The printer's sheet lines at the foot of every eighth page ("Philos.-histor. Abh. 1905. IV. N") and the signature marks ("2*", "3*" …) are normalised and not discussed here.

**Terms that vary between sections.** The translation was made in batches, and three renderings drift. *Gottheit* appears as "deity" in the earlier part (about pp. 88–107) and as "Godhead" in the later (about pp. 108–127; e.g. pp. 116, 124). *Einigkeit* is "concord" in pp. 94 and 98 but "oneness" from about p. 108 on, distinct from *Einheit* "unity" and *Vereinigung* "union". *Schwärmer* / *Schwärmerei* is "visionaries" on p. 90 but "enthusiast(s)" / "enthusiasm" on pp. 119 and 127. Readers comparing these passages should treat the pairs as the same German word. Other fixed choices: *Erlebnis* "lived experience", *Gemüt* "heart", *Gesinnung* "disposition", *Sittlichkeit* "ethical life", *Gemütszustände* "states of the heart", *Bund* "covenant" in Hegel's own usage. — prepared from the production records, Oct 2026

## [p. 19] Schelling's letter; Lessing's confidant

## [p. 34] "Tiefere Stufe der Sittlichkeit"
Dilthey calls abstract morality a *tiefere Stufe* of ethical life. The German is ambiguous between "deeper" and "lower"; the edition reads "lower stage", abstract morality standing beneath ethical life realised in the state. — prepared from the production records, Oct 2026

## [p. 102] Hegel's difficult phrases
*Es hat es aufgegeben, wie sein Objekt angetastet wurde* is read "it gave it up as soon as its object was assailed" (*wie* = as soon as). *Ebensoviel im Gebiete gewonnen* is rendered literally. "Forgiveness of one's own faults" takes *seine* as the forgiver's, not Jesus'. — prepared from the production records, Oct 2026

## [p. 107] "Um des Toten willen"
*Es herrscht die Liebe um des Toten willen* is "Love prevails for the sake of what is dead", *des Toten* being the dead thing, that is matter. The sentence ends the page ("For him matter is") and continues on p. 108 with "absolute." (*Die Materie ist für ihn absolut*). — prepared from the production records, Oct 2026

## [p. 131] Hegel on the perishable and the eternal
