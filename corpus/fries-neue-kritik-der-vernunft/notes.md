## General remark — Terminology and emphasis
Fixed renderings: *Kraft* = power, *Vermögen* = faculty, *Gemüth* and *Geist* = mind (*Geist* = spirit where opposed to matter or in the defining passage at I, p. 7), *Willkühr* = choice, *willkührlich* = voluntary (kept as "arbitrary" only where the sense is plainly arbitrariness, e.g. pp. XLIX and 24), *Erklärung* in the logical sense = definition, *Nachweisung* = showing or exhibiting, *Wiederbewußtseyn* = re-consciousness, *Wissenschaftslehre* = *Doctrine of Science*, *Schwärmer* = fanatics, *Dichtung* = invention (or fiction), *Aufhebung* = removal or cancellation (never "sublation"), *Satz vom Grunde* = principle of ground. *Grundsatz* is rendered "basic principle" only where Fries explicitly contrasts it with *Princip* (e.g. p. 181), elsewhere "principle". A few renderings are not uniform across the book: *Erkenntnißkraft* (cognitive power / power of cognition), *Kombination* (composition / combination), *schlechthin* (absolutely / simply), *geistig* (spiritual in vol. II pp. 151–167, mental in pp. 204–209). Words the print letter-spaces (Sperrdruck) are italicised, and only the words actually spaced; Antiqua (roman-type) words in the Fraktur text and the titles of works are italicised too. Names are modernised (Cartesius = Descartes, Jakobi = Jacobi, Humisch = Humean, Wolfisch = Wolffian, Erhard Schmidt = Erhard Schmid, Friedrich II. = Frederick II); Kant's and others' quotations are translated afresh from the German as Fries gives them. Greek is kept as printed (without accents where the print has none). — prepared from the production records, Oct 2026

## General remark — Handwriting in the copy and sentences split by page breaks
The scanned copy carries occasional handwritten marginalia, among them additions of the form "Zusatz … 3. Thl." referring to a third part (vol. I pp. 179 and 225; vol. II pp. 94, 299, 312). They are ignored and the printed text is translated. Two hand corrections which supply an evidently omitted or mistaken word are adopted, at vol. I p. 336 and vol. II p. 269 (see there). Where German verb-final order or a hyphenated word runs over a page break, the English moves a word or two to the neighbouring page so that each page reads as a sentence; nothing is dropped or repeated at the joins. — prepared from the production records, Oct 2026
