# GLOSSARY and policies — *Bolzano's Wissenschaftslehre und Religionswissenschaft in einer beurtheilenden Uebersicht* (Sulzbach 1841)

Binding for translators and reviewers. The book is an anonymous "critical overview" of Bolzano's
*Wissenschaftslehre* (4 vols., 1837) and *Lehrbuch der Religionswissenschaft* (4 vols., 1834). The writer speaks as
"wir" (= "we"), calls Bolzano "B." and the author of the reviewed work "der Vf." / "der Verf.".

## Titles and running elements
- Book title: *Bolzano's Theory of Science and Science of Religion in a Critical Overview* (subtitle: "A Work for All
  Who Wish to Become Acquainted with His Most Important Views").
- Part titles: "I. Ueberſicht der Wiſſenſchaftslehre." → "I. Overview of the Theory of Science."
  "II. Ueberſicht der Religionswiſſenſchaft." → "II. Overview of the Science of Religion."
- Sheet line at the foot of pp. 17, 33, 49 … ("Beurtheilende Ueberſicht üb. Bolzanos Wiſſenſchaftslehre.  N") →
  `<p class="sigf" …><span class="t">Critical Overview of Bolzano's Theory of Science.</span><span class="f">N</span></p>`.
- Star signatures ("1*", "2*" …) kept exactly as printed.

## Quotations, titles, references
- Quotations (from Bolzano, Hegel, Beneke, reviewers, the Bible …): translate fresh and precisely, with the terms above.
  Bible verses fresh from the German, not from an English Bible.
- Quotation marks: the print opens EVERY line of a quotation with „ (printed ,,). Do not reproduce that. English: “ at
  the start of the quotation, ” at its end (inner ‚…‘ → ‘…’). A page that begins inside a quotation starts with “ (this
  mirrors the print's line-initial marks); a new paragraph inside a quotation also starts with “.
- Titles of works: keep the print's quotation marks where it has them; italics only where the print is letter-spaced.
  Translate titles of books (Athanasia stays *Athanasia* only where spaced); keep names of journals in German
  (e.g. Leipziger Literaturzeitung).
- References: "§. 54." → "§ 54"; "§§. 379—384." → "§§ 379–384"; "S. 330—350" → "pp. 330–350"; "S. 37" → "p. 37";
  "Bd. I." → "vol. I"; "Bde." → "vols."; "2te Aufl." → "2nd ed."; "Nr." → "no."; "Z." → "l."; Bible "1 Theſſ. 5, 17."
  → "1 Thess. 5:17"; spans with an en dash; the print's "§. 280. u. 1." / "Nr. 185 u. 6." = §§ 280 and 281 / nos. 185 and 186 (write the numbers out).
- Abbreviations: B. → B. (B.'s → B.'s) · der Vf./Verf. → the author · Hr. Prof. X / Hrn. Prof. X → Professor X ·
  Dr. → Dr. · u. ſ. w. → etc. · z. B. → e.g. · d. h. / d. i. → i.e. · u. dgl. → and the like · vgl. → cf. · ſ. → see ·
  a. a. O. → loc. cit.
- Names: modern standard forms in the English (Kanten/Hegeln → Kant/Hegel; Leibnitz → Leibniz); initials as printed.
- Latin: short phrases kept in italics (+ [gloss] if not obvious), e.g. *Sophisma pigrum* [the lazy sophism],
  *in inf.* (= in infinitum). Variables (A, B, P, S, x) as printed, upright.
- American spelling (judgment, favor, analyze). Keep the writer's long periods, rhetorical questions and dashes.
- Misprints: translate the evident sense and mention the reading in your notes; do not "correct" folios, signatures or
  the author's opinions.
