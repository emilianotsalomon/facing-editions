# GLOSSARY and policies — Brentano, *Die Psychologie des Aristoteles* (Mainz 1867)
Binding for translators and reviewers.

## Title and parts
*Die Psychologie des Aristoteles, insbesondere seine Lehre vom νοῦς ποιητικός* → *The Psychology of Aristotle, in
Particular His Doctrine of the νοῦς ποιητικός*. Einleitung → Introduction; Erster/Zweiter Abschnitt → First/Second
Section; Erster…Vierter Theil → First…Fourth Part; Rückblick → Retrospect; Schlussbetrachtungen → Concluding
Reflections; Beilage → Appendix. Running numbered paragraphs ("1.", "2." at a paragraph start) stay as printed.

## Policies
- Greek and Latin quotations stay exactly as printed in BOTH files; do not translate them (as in the Trendelenburg
  edition). German glosses inside them — "(näml. τῆς αἰσθητικῆς)", "(Andere lesen ἰδίῳ)" — are translated. Single
  Greek words in German sentences stay Greek. Greek/Latin misprints: an evident non-word → standard form; a real word
  or variant → keep as printed.
- Quotations from German authors (Trendelenburg, Zeller, Brandis, Ritter, Kampe …) translated fresh; French or
  Italian quoted in the text stays as printed.
- Titles of works stay in the language printed (De Anim., Metaph., Anal. Post., Eth. Nic., Geschichte der griech.
  Philosophie …). Aristotle's works named in German prose are translated: "die Bücher von der Seele" → "the books On
  the Soul"; "die Metaphysik" → "the Metaphysics".
- References: keep their printed form ("De Anim. III, 5. §. 2. p. 430, a, 14.") and translate only the words:
  Vgl./vgl. → Cf./cf.; ebend. → ibid.; a. a. O. → loc. cit.; S. → p.; Anm./A. → n.; Z. → l.; Cap. → chap.;
  Buch → book; näml. → namely; u. s. w. → etc.; f./ff. stay; s. o./s. u. → see above/below; v. u./v. o. → from
  bottom/top; Bd. → vol.; Aufl. → ed.; Uebers. → transl.
- Names modernised in the English only: Thomas von Aquin → Thomas Aquinas; Theophrast → Theophrastus; Alexander von
  Aphrodisias → Alexander of Aphrodisias; Plato stays Plato; Averroes, Avicenna, Themistius, Simplicius,
  Philoponus, Zabarella as such.
- Emphasis: letter-spaced (Sperrdruck) or italic words → `<em>`, in both files, also spaced Greek. Bold display
  type in headings is not emphasis.
- Spelling: American. German column: orthography exactly as printed (Theil, Ueberblick, blos, wesshalb, Princip …),
  printed misprints kept (e.g. "Aristoteliscken" in the Contents); `,,` is the OCR's „ ; write „…“.
- ERRATA (printed on p. 252, s283) — apply them silently in the ENGLISH ONLY (German column stays as printed),
  noting each in your notes file:
  p. 3 (s034) n. 6: the words "Aehnlich u. s. w." belong at the end of note 4, not in note 6 (move their English);
  p. 88 (s119) l. 19 from bottom: αἴτιον → αἰτίαν; p. 98 (s129) l. 3 from bottom: ὥσπερ ὁρᾶν → ὥσπερ — ὁρᾶν;
  p. 104 (s135) l. 22 from bottom: "kurzen Worten" → "kurzem Worte" (translate the corrected reading);
  p. 149 (s180) l. 10 from bottom: Metaph. I, 1. → Metaph. Z.; p. 169 (s200) l. 7 from bottom: b, 6. → a, 6.;
  p. 181 (s212) l. 8 from top: "dort, der" → "dort der".
- Footnote numbers are the printer's (numbered per Section/Part, up to ~340; restarting in the Appendix): copy them
  exactly, never renumber.
- (Added after the batches) Where Brentano's "n." refers to his own numbered paragraphs ("Theil III. n. 3", "vgl. n. 13.")
  the English writes "no." (Part III, no. 3); "Anm." / "A." (footnote) stays "n.". Run-together footnotes printed on
  one line ("86) … — 87) …") are split into separate note blocks (the dash dropped) — accepted.
