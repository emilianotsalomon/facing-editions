# Glossary and policies — Hegel, *Encyklopädie der philosophischen Wissenschaften im Grundrisse* (Heidelberg 1817)
# → *Encyclopaedia of the Philosophical Sciences in Outline*

BINDING for translators and reviewers. Where this file is silent, choose the plain, exact scholarly English of the
Cambridge Hegel Translations (Brinkmann/Dahlstrom *Encyclopaedia Logic*, di Giovanni *Science of Logic*, Inwood
*Philosophy of Mind*) and note the decision in your notes file. Never paste or imitate a published translation
(Taubeneck's 1990 version of this 1817 text is copyrighted): translate fresh from the print.

## General policies
- **Register**: Hegel's compressed lecture-handbook German. Render it exactly: keep his sentence structure, his
  dashes ( — ), parentheses, semicolons and the order of clauses wherever English can bear them. No explanatory
  additions, no smoothing into easier claims, no condensing. When a sentence is genuinely ambiguous, keep the
  ambiguity in English if possible; otherwise pick the reading the context supports and note it.
- **Faithfulness first**: case endings decide who does what to whom — check subject/object carefully; keep every
  "nicht", "nur", "erst", "selbst", "eben", "vielmehr", "zugleich", "an sich" etc. Hegel's "nicht … sondern" and
  "nur … nicht" are where sense errors happen.
- **Sperrdruck** (letter-spaced words) → `<em>` exactly where printed (also spaced names). Roman-type (Antiqua) words
  in a German sentence (Latin, French, Greek terms): `<em>`.
- **Paragraph numbers**: as printed, e.g. `§. 4.` (keep the period after "§"). Cross-references in the text:
  "(§. 12.)" → "(§ 12)"; "vgl. §. 40" → "cf. § 40"; "§§. 20–22" → "§§ 20–22".
- **Latin**: short phrases kept in Latin in `<em>`, with `[English]` the first time on a page when the sense is not
  obvious (*causa sui* needs none; *a posteriori*, *a priori*, *per se*, *ens rationis* none).
  Greek: exactly as printed. French: translate, keep a technical French term in `<em>` if Hegel quotes it as such.
- **Quotations** (Kant, Jacobi, Fichte, Schelling, Spinoza, Newton, Goethe, Bible…): translate FRESH from the German
  as Hegel prints it, with the terms below. Quotation marks „…“ → “…”, inner ‚…‘ → ‘…’.
- **Divine pronouns**: follow the print (Er → He, er → he).
- **Names**: modernised in the English: Göthe → Goethe, Leibnitz → Leibniz, Cartesius → Descartes, Aristoteles →
  Aristotle, Plato(n) → Plato, Keppler → Kepler, Newton stays, Jakob Böhme stays, Spinoza stays.
- **Spelling**: American (color, analyze, center, defense, judgment), but the title keeps "Encyclopaedia".
- **Abbreviations**: "u. s. f." / "u. s. w." → "etc."; "d. i." / "d. h." → "i.e."; "z. B." → "e.g."; "vgl." → "cf.";
  "S." (page) → "p."; "a. a. O." → "loc. cit.".
- **Capitalisation**: Concept, Idea, Object (= Objekt in the Logic of the Concept) are capitalised; spirit, nature,
  reason, being, essence, the absolute, God (as printed) — lower case except God.
