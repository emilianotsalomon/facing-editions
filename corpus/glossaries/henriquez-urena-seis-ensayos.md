# Glossary and conventions — Henríquez Ureña, *Seis ensayos en busca de nuestra expresión* (binding)

## Titles (use exactly)
- Seis ensayos en busca de nuestra expresión → *Six Essays in Search of Our Expression*
- I. Orientaciones → I. Orientations · II. Figuras → II. Figures
- El descontento y la promesa → Discontent and Promise
- Caminos de nuestra historia literaria → Paths of Our Literary History
- Hacia el nuevo teatro → Toward the New Theater
- Don Juan Ruiz de Alarcón / Enrique González Martínez / Alfonso Reyes → unchanged
- Dos apuntes argentinos → Two Argentine Notes
- El amigo argentino → The Argentine Friend · Poesía argentina contemporánea → Contemporary Argentine Poetry
  · (La antología de Julio Noé) → (Julio Noé’s Anthology)
- Panorama de la otra América → Panorama of the Other America
- Veinte años de literatura en los Estados Unidos → Twenty Years of Literature in the United States
- Palabras finales → Closing Words
- Subheadings (bold): translate plainly, title case (La independencia literaria → Literary Independence).

## Recurring terms
- nuestra América → our America (Martí’s phrase; lower-case “our”)
- la América española / hispánica → Spanish America; hispanoamericano → Spanish American;
  América (the continent) → America; americano (= of the Americas, usually Spanish America) → American
  (when PHU means Spanish American and context is ambiguous, still “American”: he is deliberate)
- la otra América (= the US) → the other America
- expresión, nuestra expresión → expression, our expression
- criollo → creole (adj./noun, lower-case); criollismo, criollista(s) → *criollismo*, *criollista(s)* (italic)
- europeizante(s) → Europeanizer(s) / Europeanizing; hispanizante(s) → Hispanizer(s)
- americanismo → Americanism
- modernismo, modernista(s) → *modernismo*, *modernista(s)* (italic; NOT English “modernism”)
- romanticismo / romántico → Romanticism / romantic (capital R for the movement)
- el descontento y la promesa → discontent and promise
- indio → Indian; indígena → Indigenous / native (follow sense)
- gaucho, gauchesco → gaucho, gauchesque; cielitos, payadas, etc. → keep Spanish in italics
- ingenio (17th-c. playwright) → wit; comedia (Golden Age) → comedia (italic) or “play”
- Siglo(s) de Oro → Golden Age · conquistadores → conquistadors · la Colonia → the colonial period
- Don / D. → Don · Fray → Fray · Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz → Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz
- teatro → theater (American spelling throughout: theater, color, program, fulfill)
- escenario → stage; decoración → scenery/set; director de escena → stage director; puesta en escena → staging

## Quotation and style rules
- Spanish « » → “ ”; inner quotes ‘ ’. Em dash — without spaces. Ellipsis … kept.
- Quotations from English-language authors (Shakespeare, Whitman, Poe, Emerson, James, Santayana, Mencken,
  Lewis, Frost, Sandburg, Lindsay, Masters, etc.): restore the ORIGINAL ENGLISH wording when you are confident of it;
  otherwise translate faithfully from the Spanish. Never invent “original” wording.
- English-language book titles: give the original English title (*Main Street*, *Spoon River Anthology*).
- Spanish/French/Latin/Italian titles of works: keep as printed, in italics, untranslated.
- Quotations in French/Latin/Italian/Portuguese: keep as printed, then add [English] in square brackets.
- Spanish verse quotations: translate line for line (one English line per Spanish line), unrhymed, faithful;
  keep the italics if printed in italics.
- Proper names: modernise/standardize spelling in the English only (Montalván → Montalbán, Berard → Bérard,
  Odysée → Odyssée when clearly a misprint). Keep all bibliographic data.
- Footnotes: translate fully. “N. del E.” → “Ed. note.” Keep the * or number mark exactly.
- Register: PHU’s prose is elegant, compressed, aphoristic, often ironic. Keep sentence shapes, rhetorical
  questions, exclamations, lists, semicolons. No modernizing slang, no explanatory additions.
