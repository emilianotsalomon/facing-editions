# Varona, *Seis conferencias* — binding glossary & policies

## Policies (the user chose these)
- **Quotations from English-language authors** (Emerson above all; also e.g. Shakespeare, Byron, Milton, Carlyle,
  Spencer, Darwin, Mill, Bain, Longfellow, Franklin, Channing, Carlyle): RESTORE the author's original English
  wherever it can be found. Use WebSearch/WebFetch (Project Gutenberg, emersoncentral.com, archive.org, Wikisource)
  and quote the original verbatim; if Varona abridges or paraphrases, quote the matching original words and keep his
  cuts (mark omissions with … as he does). If the source cannot be found with reasonable effort (≈3 searches),
  translate from Varona's Spanish and say so in your notes. Record every source found (work, chapter) in notes.
- **Quotations from authors in other languages** (Homer, Plato, Scudéry, Hugo, Michelet, Cervantes, Montaigne,
  Rousseau, Taine, Renan…): translate FRESH from Varona's Spanish — do not substitute a published English translation,
  since Varona's wording is often his own. Exception: verse or phrases he gives in the ORIGINAL language
  (French, Latin, Italian) stay as printed, with an English gloss in [square brackets] after them.
- **Latin phrases**: keep the Latin (italic as printed) + [English] in square brackets, e.g.
  *Domi mansit, lanam fecit* [She kept the house, she spun the wool]. Common tags already English usage
  (a priori, etc.) need no gloss.
- **Proper names: modernised in English** standard form: Mlle. de Scudéry (print: Scudery), Victor Hugo,
  Plato, Homer, Telemachus, Penelope, Michelet, Cervantes, Don Quixote, Sancho Panza, Dulcinea, Rocinante,
  Napoleon, Louis Bonaparte, Montaigne, Molière, Rousseau, Fénelon, Mme. de Maintenon, Mme. de Sévigné, Mme. de
  Rambouillet, Saint-Cyr, Concord, Massachusetts, Boston, Harvard. Where the English form is unclear, use the
  person's own national spelling.
- **Titles of works**: italic. Known works under their usual original/English title — *Don Quixote*, *Les
  Châtiments*, *Napoléon le Petit*, *La Légende des siècles*, *Les Misérables*, *Clélie*, *Artamène, ou le Grand
  Cyrus*, *L'Amour*, *La Femme*, the *Symposium*, *Phaedrus*; Emerson's *Nature*, *Essays*, *Representative Men*,
  *English Traits*, *The Conduct of Life*, *Society and Solitude*, “The American Scholar”, “Self-Reliance”, “The
  Over-Soul”. If unsure, translate the title Varona gives and note it.
- **Divine pronouns**: follow the print (Dios → God; Él/él → He/he as printed).
- ¿ ¡ dropped. « » → “ ”. British or American spelling? → **American** spelling throughout (honor, labor, center),
  but keep “-ise/-ize” as “-ize”.
- Running heads translated as printed (“SEIS CONFERENCIAS” → Six Lectures; “ENRIQUE JOSÉ VARONA” stays). Folios:
  exactly the number printed on the scan, even if odd.
- Footnote dates: “9 de diciembre de 1882” → “December 9, 1882”. Institutions keep their Spanish name in italic
  where italic in the print (*Nuevo Liceo de la Habana*); otherwise translate the generic part: “el teatro de Payret”
  → “the Teatro de Payret”; “la Sociedad Antropológica” → “the Anthropological Society”.
