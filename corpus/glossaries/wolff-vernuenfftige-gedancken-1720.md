# GLOSSARY — Wolff, *Vernünfftige Gedancken von GOtt, der Welt und der Seele des Menschen* (Halle 1720)

Binding for all batch translators. Where a term has a fixed rendering, use it every time, including in marginal glosses, running heads and the two Registers. Reference vocabulary: Dyck (OUP 2019), Blackwall, Leibniz in Loemker/Ariew–Garber, and Baumgarten (Fugate & Hymers). Never copy sentences from any of them. The user's earlier volume, *Reasonable Thoughts on the Purposes of Natural Things*, already uses **concept, ground, actuality, force, purpose, Mr von Leibniz, "On …" chapter titles**, and this volume keeps them.

## 4. Conventions and style

- **GOtt / GOttes** → God / God's. Use divine pronouns in lower case mid-sentence (he, his, him), following the user's Tiberghien decision.
- **Herr von Leibnitz / Leibniz** → **Mr von Leibniz** (no full stop after "Mr", as in the *Purposes* volume). Modernise all proper names in the English only. Cartesius → Descartes; Newtonus → Newton.
- **Cross-references:** keep the printed form of the § sign, e.g. "(§. 45)", "(§. 423. 427)", "(§. 2. c. 1. Log.)", "c. 4."; translate only what is plainly German ("u. f." → "ff."; "& seqq." stays). "Log." = German Logic, "Met." = this book. Keep the Second Index's page numbers exactly as printed.
- **Footnotes** "(d)" etc. keep their letters. Latin book titles inside them stay Latin ("Elementis Mechanicæ").
- "z. E." → "e.g."; "d. i." / "das ist" → "that is"; "u. s. w." → "etc."
- **Marginal glosses:** translate them as short headings in the same register ("Wie wir erkennen, daß wir sind." → "How we know that we are.").
- **Paragraph heads:** keep "§. 1." exactly as printed, including the full stop after §.
- Wolff's emphasis (bold or larger Fraktur for defined terms, e.g. "ein **Gantzes**") → *italics* in English.
- Antiqua-set Latin or French words in the German (citationes, Maxime) stay as printed in roman. Translate German loan forms naturally ("communiciret" → communicated).
- **Register:** plain, clear early-18th-century prose. No "thee/thou", no "doth". "Allein" at sentence start → "But"; "Derowegen / Demnach" → "Therefore / Accordingly"; "Solchergestalt" → "In this way".
- **Sentences:** keep Wolff's long chains of reasoning (da … so …), but split a German sentence when English would otherwise become unreadable. Never drop the logical connectives (denn, daher, also, folglich).
- **Defined terms:** keep the term fixed within a definition and its sequel. If Wolff repeats "Krafft", the English repeats "force". Do not vary for elegance.
- **"vor"** in period German often means "für": "vor sich bestehend" → subsisting by itself; "vor etwas halten" → take for.
- **"Mensch"** → man. "menschlich" → human. "der Mensch" in generic statements → "man" (no article).
- Fixed principles: **principle of contradiction**, **principle of sufficient reason**, **principle of indiscernibles**, **pre-established harmony**, **physical influx**, **occasional causes**.
- Keep **Grund (ground) / Ursache (cause) / Bewegungs-Grund (motive) / Absicht (purpose)** strictly apart. Wolff's argument depends on this.
- Keep **Krafft (force) / Vermögen (faculty) / Macht (power)** strictly apart.
- Animal-related terms: "Thiere" → animals. "Abrichtung der Thiere" → training of animals. "Vernunfft-ähnliches" → analogue of reason.
- Quotations from English authors (Locke, Newton, Boyle) are rare here. If one appears, restore the original English where it can be found.
