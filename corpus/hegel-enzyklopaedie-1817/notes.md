## General remark — Editorial policies
**Translation.** The English is made afresh from the 1817 print, keeping Hegel's compressed syntax, dashes, parentheses and clause order wherever English can bear them; no published English version is followed. Quotations (Kant, Jacobi, Fichte, Schelling, Spinoza, Newton, Goethe and others) are likewise translated afresh. American spelling is used; names are modernised (Göthe → Goethe, Leibnitz → Leibniz, Keppler → Kepler, Guiton Morveau → Guyton Morveau).

**Terms.** *Begriff* is "Concept", *Idee* "Idea", and *Objekt* (in the Logic of the Concept and in the Philosophy of Spirit) "Object", whereas *Gegenstand* is "object". *Aufheben* is "sublate" (*Aufheben* "sublation"). *Vorstellung* is "representation", *Verstand* "understanding", *Erkennen* "cognition", *Verhältniß* "relationship" and *Beziehung* "relation", *Gesinnung* "disposition", *Eigenwille* "self-will", *Lebendigkeit* "vitality", *Diremtion* "diremption", *Entäusserung* "externalization" and *Äusserung* "expression". *Selbstständigkeit* is "independence" (*unselbstständig* "dependent"). *Gehalt* is "import" (not "substance", which is *Substanz*). *Reflexion-in-sich* is "reflection-into-itself". *Daseyn* is "determinate being" in the technical sense of the Logic. *Sache* is "matter" in general but "thing" in the treatment of Right (property, contract, from § 403 on, p. 260). Spirit, nature, reason, being, essence and the absolute are in lower case; only Concept, Idea and Object are capitalised.

**The figures of the syllogism.** The print's letters E—B—A (*Einzelnheit*, *Besonderheit*, *Allgemeinheit*) are rendered S—P—U (singular, particular, universal) throughout, with the order of the letters carried over (A—E—B becomes U—S—P).

**Print features kept.** Paragraph numbers and the slips in their sequence stand as printed (see pp. 11, 20, 32, 46, 92–93, 96–97, 125, 165–167). Folio misprints are kept (pp. 114, 222). Letter-spaced type (Sperrdruck) and Antiqua words in the German are rendered in italics where the print has them. On the faint UC scan, spacing of a word could sometimes not be told from justification spacing, and a few calls are doubtful (e.g. "concrete" on p. 81, "light" on p. 174, "earth" on p. 181).

**Headings.** Headings follow the wording of the Contents, with the article dropped (e.g. "Mechanism", "Chemism", "Judgment", "Philosophy"). Where the printed heading in the text differs from the Contents, the printed wording is translated (e.g. "Organic Physics", p. 180).

**Page breaks.** Where Hegel's sentence or a hyphenated word runs across a page break, the English breaks where the German does, so far as English word order permits; a word divided between pages stays with the page on which it begins. Places where English could not follow the German exactly are mentioned in the notes below.

**Translator's glosses.** Brackets in the English enclose a few glosses: for Latin phrases (pp. 24, 50, 98) and the German word "Urtheil" (p. 83). — prepared from the production records, Oct 2026

## [p. 249] What is thought
