# Glossary and policies — Trendelenburg, *Logische Untersuchungen* (3rd ed., 1870), translated chapters IX–XXIV → *Logical Investigations*

Decisions recorded during production (the translators' brief and the lists of doubtful renderings); partial. Where this file is silent, choose the plain scholarly English a careful translator of Hegel, Lotze or Brentano would use (see `_general-german-to-english.md`), and note the decision.

## Policies
- **Register**: faithful and complete, in the register of a careful nineteenth-century philosophy translation into modern scholarly English. Keep Trendelenburg's sentence architecture and his emphases where English allows; split a sentence only when English becomes unreadable. No paraphrase, summary or modernisation of his examples.
- **Titles and quotations**: titles of books stay in their original language (German, Latin, Greek, English) in the notes and in the main text (Kant's *Kritik der reinen Vernunft* stays in German). Greek and Latin quotations stay in the original, without added translations. In bibliographic notes only the connecting words are translated (Vgl. → Cf., S. → p., Bd. → vol., Aufl. → ed., u. → and; ff. stays; S. / s. = siehe in notes → See).
- **Headings**: chapter headings in capitals stay in capitals; Roman numerals and section numbers are kept.
- **Emphasis**: the emphasis on *Ein* / *Einer* (*Eines Ganzen*) is rendered as italic "*one* whole"; lower-case ein / eines is simply "a" (italic *one* only for spaced or capitalised Ein / Einer).
- **OCR**: obvious OCR slips in the German (`derGriechen`, a stray `-:`) are translated by their evident meaning, not reproduced; Greek visibly garbled by OCR is kept as it stands.
- **Spelling**: "premises" (not "premisses"); never "movement" for *Bewegung* (always "motion", also in the plural); *Inhalt* of a concept is "content", never "intension".
