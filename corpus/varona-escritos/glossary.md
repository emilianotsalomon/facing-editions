# Varona, *Escritos* (Cultura, 1917) — binding glossary & policies

## Policies (the user chose these)
- **Quotations from English-language authors** (Byron above all — “Como Byron”; also e.g. Shakespeare, Milton,
  Carlyle, Spencer, Darwin, Mill, Emerson, Poe, Wilde, Kipling): RESTORE the author's original English wherever it can
  be found (WebSearch/WebFetch: Project Gutenberg, Wikisource, archive.org) and quote it verbatim; keep Varona's cuts
  (…). If not found after ≈3 searches, translate from Varona's Spanish and say so in your notes. Record sources found.
- **Quotations from authors in other languages** (Rostand, French/Italian/German/Yiddish/Hebrew authors, classical
  authors): translate FRESH from Varona's Spanish. Verse or phrases he gives in the ORIGINAL language (French, Latin,
  Italian…) stay as printed, with an English gloss in [square brackets] after them.
- **Latin**: keep (italic as printed) + [English]. *Splendide mendax* (Horace, Odes III.11) stays as the title.
- **Proper names** modernised in English standard form (Shakespeare, Heine, Rostand, Byron, Moses…); where unclear,
  the person's own national spelling.
- **Titles of works** italic, under their usual original/English title (*Cyrano de Bergerac*, *Chantecler*,
  *L'Aiglon*, *Childe Harold*, *Don Juan*, *Manfred*); if unsure, translate Varona's title and note it.
- ¿ ¡ dropped. « » and “ ” → “ ”, inner ‘ ’. **American spelling** (honor, labor, center).
- Running heads as printed: verso “CULTURA” → Cultura (series name, not translated); recto “ENRIQUE JOSÉ VARONA”.
- “N. de los Directores” = Editors’ note (`<span class="sc">Editors’ note.</span>`); “N. del A.” = Author’s note.
- Divine pronouns: follow the print.
