## General remark — Provenance of the edition
Source: Mexico: Cultura, 1917, with a prologue by Antonio Caso (set in italic with roman emphasis), HathiTrust copy (Wisconsin), 97 pages; publisher notices and advertisements are left out. — prepared from the production records, Oct 2026

## General remark — Editorial policies
The English text follows Varona's Spanish closely; where a passage of English-language literature is quoted, the original English has been looked up and restored verbatim (Brooke, Shakespeare, Hood, de Sélincourt), while quotations from other languages are translated afresh from Varona's Spanish and, where Varona gives no gloss, Latin, French, Italian and German phrases are kept and glossed in square brackets. Titles of works are given in their original language or standard English form with a bracketed gloss (e.g. *La Rôtisserie des poètes* [The Poets' Cookshop]). Names are modernised and obvious slips of the print are corrected silently (*Coussine* to *Cousine*, *Bernier* to *Bornier*, *Rozenfeld* to Rosenfeld, *Kischineff* to Kishinev, *Elizabeth Browning* to Elizabeth Barrett Browning, *Bounin* to Bunin, "Manua" to Brooke's "Mamua"), and French accents lost in the print are restored ("homme à femmes"; "être à moi / à personne" in *Cyrano*, Act II). The prologue by Antonio Caso is set in italic, with roman type for emphasis. Where a hyphenated Spanish word falls at a page break, it is completed on the page where it begins, so that sentences may split at a slightly different point in the English than in the Spanish. Terms of office and institutions in the address are rendered as follows: *consejo de secretarios del Despacho* "council of the Secretaries of the Cabinet", *burladero* "bullring barrier", *renta* "revenue", *valla de gallos* "cockpit", *norteamericanas* "American". Publisher's notices and advertisements are omitted.
— prepared from the production records, Oct 2026

## [p. 6] Roman emphasis in the prologue
In Caso's italic prologue, only "como especialidad en generalidades" (rendered "as a specialty in generalities") is roman, not the preceding word. Scattered single roman letters in the print are font quirks and are ignored. Other roman words in the prologue include "the spiral of his own mental transformations", "Father Juanín.", "gentleman", "diverse, undulating," and "Candide". At the foot of the page the English ends "nuanced," completing the word *matiza-da*.
— prepared from the production records, Oct 2026

## [p. 7] Negation and wording in the prologue
"Que no han logrado, sino por excepción, el secreto…" had lost its negation and is now "who have not achieved, save by exception, the secret". "Sin amargura, trágica, pero con sincera emoción" is taken as "without tragic bitterness, but with sincere emotion"; *escritores americanos* is "writers of the Americas" and *latino americanos* "Latin American".
— prepared from the production records, Oct 2026

## [p. 13] The painter Bunin
The painter whom Varona calls "Bounin" is Narkiz Nikolaevich Bunin (1856–1912), whose *Fishing* was shown at the Passazh exhibition in St. Petersburg in March 1903 with Tolstoy as the central figure. Varona's identification of the right-hand figure (as Gorky) is left as printed, although the auction note names Repin. "Droit de cité" is translated "charter of citizenship", with the French kept in parentheses as in the print. The hyphenated "Gor-ki" is completed on this page.

## [p. 30] The de Sélincourt quotation
The passage quoted by Varona from Ernest de Sélincourt, *English Poets and the National Ideal* (Oxford, 1915), Lecture II, "Milton", p. 34, was found and is restored verbatim: "we view the contest as a struggle between the ideals of military ambition, which recognizes no right but force, and of free and unimpeded national development. It is true that to Germany the war appears in something of the same light." Varona's parenthetical "(in something of the same light)" would now duplicate the text, so it is dropped and the phrase kept in italics as in the print. The quotation continues onto p. 31.
