## Why I Became a Magazine Editor

By José Vasconcelos

*Books Abroad*, 1932

*[Note on the text: OCR'd from *Books Abroad* (via JSTOR). Trailing page furniture and the unrelated review that followed in the original issue ("Boulanger and Hitler," by Albert Guérard) have been omitted.]*

I take advantage of the hospitality of this excellent publication, *Books Abroad*, in order to say a few words about my purpose in establishing my new review *La Antorcha*. I have been painted often as an anti-foreigner, a racial patriot over-zealous for the interests of Mexico and Spanish America. If I were an American citizen, I should be a cosmopolitan pacifist and an internationalist. The nationally strong have a right to be that; in fact, such a position is their duty. But when you belong with the downtrodden, the exploited and the weak, then it is almost cowardice to talk of calmness, peace and internationalism. I have opposed the American political influence in Mexico because it has always been exercised for the benefit of the big business interests, and for the behoof of a few Mexican traitors whose conduct is that of despots and military dictators.

I should welcome any American influence that was sincere, and any influence that tried to help us emerge from our barbarous militarism instead of allying itself with murder and theft. I should be grateful to a brand of American sympathy that would desire to see Mexicans enjoy a part of the advantages of good government, decency and freedom that are desired by any American at home. Many of us have been distressed by seeing how the people of the United States, who have enjoyed living in comfort in their own country, show toward the Mexican people, not good-will, but sympathy with elements that make for our destruction, for the corruption of our national life, at the hands of incompetent representatives, of men who are tools of the interests which do not labor for the furtherance of good-will among men. And it is against this sort of representation, against this sort of help, and against this dishonest propaganda that I have revolted. And to oppose it, and to denounce it, and to clear the minds of my race all over Spanish America, I have started this *Antorcha*, the Torch. May it not only give light, but also set fire to a condition of iniquity, to a situation of distrust and destruction, that is undermining the future of the whole American continent.

In my publication I am trying to deal with facts, that is to say, with facts that reveal, that confirm certain conditions and theories. I tried my best, after the recent Presidential elections in Mexico, to find in North America an organ which would tell the truth about conditions in Mexico and the interference of Americans there. I found no paper, no review, that was willing to give my version, even at the cost of criticizing it. I decided, then, to make a trip to the south, to tell the truth by word of mouth, among my people of the Spanish American race. I succeeded in getting the ear of nations. I found hearers and I found support, popular support, support given to me even against the will of certain political parties. My lectures made it possible for me not only to travel, but also to create my *Antorcha* in Paris.

Why did I choose Paris? Ask those Spanish American governments that murder editors, ask in Mexico, ask in Cuba, why it is not possible to maintain an independent and frank publication among us. There are of course certain cities of Spanish America that would [p. 8] have allowed my activities and would even have welcomed them, but the problem of communication was another serious difficulty to surmount. Then again, facilities for printing, etc., are much better in this ancient center of learning and freedom, Paris, beloved of the nations.

This is why I am here; and from here and from Madrid, the recently liberated metropolis of our race, I expect to continue writing, agitating.

I tell my people the exact truth, particularly about those matters which are either not mentioned or are misrepresented by the big news agencies, dominated as they are by imperialistic and private interests. I preach and propagate those social, moral, philosophical doctrines that tend to keep us or to make us free, as free as the North American was before imperialism and prohibition.

When they tell me that I preach hate and anti-Americanism, I think of the masses of the North American people, among whom I have lived, and I feel that the destruction of imperialism and of governments in subjection to the selfish interests of bankers, would be a blessing even for them. I do not preach Communism, because I do not believe in it. I believe in liberty, I believe in a system of economics adapted to our vast national resources in America. I believe in the political economy best suited to the exploitation of our vast national resources, whether state economy or collective economy; not, of course, trust economy. I believe in the future of the New World, and I trust that that future will bring us together on the basis of sincerity and fair play, both Americas of us, the northern and the southern America. We shall then be friends, after the wrong-doers are punished. We shall be warm friends, even if now we must have the appearance of enemies and must fight each other with all the weapons of desperation and darkness.

In the world of today, all races and all nations are awakening and uniting, striving to construct powerful confederacies and kingdoms. The Anglo-Saxons started this movement when they began to build that powerful world entity, the "English speaking world." The Russians, the Orientals, the Mussulmans, every people with a spark of consciousness, are organizing for the future, consolidating their positions, consolidating each its collective soul. Why should not Latin-Americans do [p. 9] the same thing? Why should the descendants of the first civilized world empire, Spain, not feel the necessity of coordinating the various nations and branches that still constitute a related group? There is no more homogeneous race than ours from the Rio Grande to El Plata, anywhere in the world.

And no other race is in more urgent need of enlightenment, guidance, organization. One hundred and twenty millions of people of free blood will not be kept politically and economically subdued, even if we have traitors in high office in our midst. Natural biological growth is constantly working in our favor. And the sooner we organize this strong human flow, the better for the peace of the continent, the better for ourselves and for the rest of the world.

(At the request of the Editor, I append an autobiographical statement:

I was born in southern Mexico. I was graduated in law and philosophy at the National University in Mexico City. My graduating thesis was a study of Pythagoras, the Greek philosopher. I had started practicing law when the Madero movement to overthrow despotism was initiated.

I was with Madero from the start, and thus acquired political prominence when Obregón and Calles, though older than myself, were obscure and unknown. When Madero became president, I went back to my law practice.

When Madero was murdered, I joined the liberating movement headed by Carranza. Carranza appointed me envoy to the international Niagara Falls conference, and later gave me an important position in education.

When Carranza made himself dictator, I opposed him, joined armed rebellion against him, was defeated and left Mexico. I lived then one year in New York, two years in South America, and then again two years in California. During this period I wrote several books on literary and philosophical subjects.

When Carranza was overthrown I became Rector of the National University and from that office I created under the De la Huerta government the Federal Department of Education. I stayed at that post four years, raising the federal budget for education from eight million to fifty million pesos.

I resigned when Obregón began to lower my budget until it went down finally, under Calles, to twenty-four million pesos. I opposed the election of Calles because it was brought about through force and because Calles is known to every Mexican to be personally unworthy. I left Mexico when Calles took office and lived in Europe, mainly Paris, for three years. While there I wrote articles for twenty of the leading newspapers of Spanish America, such as *El Universal* of Mexico City, *El Tiempo* of Bogotá, *El País* of Havana, *Crítica* of Buenos Aires, etc. In 1928, I lectured on Spanish American sociological subjects in different American Universities.

In 1929 I became a candidate for the presidency of Mexico through popular acclaim and the formal support of the only organized revolutionary party, the "Antireeleccionista." I obtained ninety-five per cent of the votes, but those in control of the election nullified the popular choice by violence and fraud.

I am now forty-nine years old. I work fourteen hours a day (as I have always done), on books that will soon appear in Madrid, books on ethics, essays, etc. I write monthly for *La Prensa* of Buenos Aires, the only newspaper engagement I retain, as my review *Antorcha* is my own organ.) — Paris.
