Compiled 12 October 2026 from web searches and the full texts or abstracts I could open. This is a working bibliography, so each entry is marked with how far I could check it:

- ✓ checked: I read the claim in the source itself (full text, abstract, or a direct quotation in a reliable secondary source). - ◐ partly checked: the work and its general argument are confirmed (catalogue record, publisher description, review), but I didn't see its treatment of Vasconcelos specifically. - ○ not checked this session: a standard reference in this debate, listed from general knowledge. Confirm it before citing.

Labels. BIO = reads the project as biological, eugenic or genetic (breeding, blood, selection, "aesthetic eugenics" as still eugenics). HOM = reads it as homogenizing (whitening, assimilation, de-Indianization, erasure of Blackness, one mestizo type absorbing the rest).

C. General frameworks often cited for the homogenizing reading (○ not checked this session)

These treat mestizaje ideology in general as homogenizing. Check each one's explicit discussion of Vasconcelos before citing.

- Guillermo Bonfil Batalla, México profundo: una civilización negada (1987): mestizaje as "de-Indianization". - Ronald Stutzman, "El Mestizaje: An All-Inclusive Ideology of Exclusion" (1981). - Antonio Cornejo Polar, "Mestizaje e hibridez: los riesgos de las metáforas" (1997): mestizaje as a harmonizing synthesis that hides conflict. - Juan E. De Castro, Mestizo Nations: Culture, Race, and Conformity in Latin American Literature (2002). - Florencia Mallon, "Constructing Mestizaje in Latin America" (1996). - Peter Wade, "Rethinking Mestizaje" (2005); Race and Ethnicity in Latin America. - Rafael Pérez-Torres, Mestizaje: Critical Uses of Race in Chicano Culture (2006). - Tace Hedrick, Mestizo Modernism (2003). - Claudio Lomnitz, Deep Mexico, Silent Mexico (2001). - Edward Telles, Pigmentocracies (2014).

Notes

1. The axis most critics dispute is "biological". Morales, Palacios and Dalton all register that Vasconcelos meant to put an aesthetic and spiritual selection in place of a biological one. The critical move (Dalton, Manrique quoting Sexton, the Dialnet article, Hernández Cuevas) is that taste-guided breeding is still eugenics. The primary text that supports this is RC pp. ~28–31 ("eugénica misteriosa del gusto estético", "podría desaparecer el negro", "extinción voluntaria"). See `mezcla-heterogeneidad-pasajes.md`, Part V. 2. The homogenizing reading usually rests on the ideology's effects as much as on the text: state indigenismo, the SEP, the census, colourism (Navarrete, Sue, Moreno Figueroa, Urías). Only Hernández Cuevas, Townsend, Sánchez Hernández and the Berkeley dissertation tie it closely to the wording of La raza cósmica. 3. The passages in your corpus that cut against both readings (Indología ¶207 "cada alma logra moldear su propia estructura externa"; Ind. p. ~81–82 against de Vries's single type; Discursos 1948 "unificación [no] supone … simplificación") are rarely cited in this literature. Hooker is the main exception: she is the critic who works most with Indología and Bolivarismo.
