# Secondary sources that read *La raza cósmica* as (1) biological and (2) homogenizing

Compiled 12 October 2026 from web searches and the full texts or abstracts I could open. This is a working bibliography, so each entry is marked with how far I could check it:

- **✓ checked**: I read the claim in the source itself (full text, abstract, or a direct quotation in a reliable secondary source).
- **◐ partly checked**: the work and its general argument are confirmed (catalogue record, publisher description, review), but I didn't see its treatment of Vasconcelos specifically.
- **○ not checked this session**: a standard reference in this debate, listed from general knowledge. Confirm it before citing.

**Labels.** **BIO** = reads the project as biological, eugenic or genetic (breeding, blood, selection, "aesthetic eugenics" as still eugenics). **HOM** = reads it as homogenizing (whitening, assimilation, de-Indianization, erasure of Blackness, one mestizo type absorbing the rest).

### Notes

1. **The axis most critics dispute is "biological".** Morales, Palacios and Dalton all register that Vasconcelos *meant* to put an aesthetic and spiritual selection in place of a biological one. The critical move (Dalton, Manrique quoting Sexton, the Dialnet article, Hernández Cuevas) is that taste-guided breeding is still eugenics. The primary text that supports this is RC pp. ~28–31 ("eugénica misteriosa del gusto estético", "podría desaparecer el negro", "extinción voluntaria"). See `mezcla-heterogeneidad-pasajes.md`, Part V.
2. **The homogenizing reading usually rests on the ideology's effects as much as on the text**: state indigenismo, the SEP, the census, colourism (Navarrete, Sue, Moreno Figueroa, Urías). Only Hernández Cuevas, Townsend, Sánchez Hernández and the Berkeley dissertation tie it closely to the wording of *La raza cósmica*.
3. **The passages in your corpus that cut against both readings** (*Indología* ¶207 "cada alma logra moldear su propia estructura externa"; Ind. p. ~81–82 against de Vries's single type; *Discursos* 1948 "unificación [no] supone … simplificación") are rarely cited in this literature. Hooker is the main exception: she is the critic who works most with *Indología* and *Bolivarismo*.
