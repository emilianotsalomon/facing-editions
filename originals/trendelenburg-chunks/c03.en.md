[[148]] That transition from the disjunctive syllogism into ⟦58⟧ objectivity, from the syllogism unfolded through all its moments into the essence that is self-sufficient through reciprocal development, may be left to rest on itself, although on closer consideration it would show itself to be untenable; for nothing in it drives outward, as objectivity demands. But we need a conceded presupposition, so that we are not compelled to unravel the threads of the fabric ever further backwards.

[[151]] Chemism is derived from the centrality of mechanism. The objects are determined against one another, and as the centre falls apart, they relate themselves to one another in reciprocal excitation. Can such a transition from the solar system, in which centrality reaches its apex, into the combinations of acids and bases, from astronomy into chemistry, be so much as divined anywhere in nature? The dialectic attempts its connections on its own account and therefore does not reach the course of coming-to-be. The thing does not come to be in this way; but perhaps the thought of the thing does, which logic undertakes to present in its eternity. That may be, although it is not to be seen why the development of the thing and the development of the thought should here fall apart in such a manner that the connection is nowhere to be seen. Mechanism and chemism have at least this in common, that in both the efficient cause reveals itself. Combination and separation occur upon external excitation. The substances seize one another and let one another go, seek one another and flee one another. The forces of things play with one another. Or are we to take it thus, that the substances, determined for one another, together realise their concept, and to see a proof of this in the definite numbers of the proportions of combination? This difference of conception is easily illustrated by a single example. We take the example from Goethe's *Wahlverwandtschaften*. If one puts a piece of lime into dilute sulphuric acid, the acid seizes the lime ⟦59⟧ and appears with it as gypsum. The proportion of combination is definite. 100 parts of sulphuric acid combine with 71 parts of lime to form gypsum. In this process, it seems, exciting forces are active, properties of the substances which meet one another and bring about compositions or separations. Then the concept nowhere appears here as though it hovered freely above the process, as though it went before it determining it. It is the process of the efficient cause, and the concept only follows it and is only drawn out of it. Such is the view if one lets the experience of chemism have its way by itself. Conversely, one would present it thus. Lime and sulphuric acid are each one-sided by themselves; they are meant to combine into gypsum as into a higher formation. The proportion of combination vouches that the concept, measuring both substances against each other, preceded the process. Then the governing end shows itself already in chemism. Gypsum is to come to be; lime and sulphuric acid are determined for this; the separate parts (lime and sulphuric acid) are to be comprehended from the thought of the whole (the gypsum). Were one to take chemism thus, it would already be teleological, and the end would then have to spring forth in the transition from mechanism to chemism. Since this does not happen, it cannot be Hegel's view; nor is it otherwise the view of science.

[[152]] From what moments, then, does the efficient cause of chemism give birth to the end? The stage of chemism negates itself in that the processes pass over into products and the products into processes, and this alternation runs on into infinity. Through this negation the concept sunk in the object becomes free for itself. But of what kind is the negation? The chemical factors (to take it more generally and not to stop at the substances of the properly chemical sphere) do indeed show their lack of independence in being thrown into a necessary process. This lack of independence is ⟦60⟧ however only a lack of power, only a dependence on another efficient cause. It is therefore a negation within this domain; the one force is limited and conditioned by the other. If we cancel this negation, the thought of the end by no means appears thereby. Since the negation of the chemical process takes place by physical means, we remain only within the efficient cause. Chemism is blind, like mechanism. How does the foreseeing end come out of it? How does the external play of combinations turn over into thought? It is nowhere shown how the concept sunk in the object is driven forth out of it, and indeed in such a way that it now is for itself and before the object, and determines its future. This reversal of the relation is nowhere grounded here, and yet the end is determined as the cause whose effect is cause! Perhaps what is missed here is placed in the infinite progress of chemism. To be sure it runs on; to be sure one can interpret it as itself, without a hold, seeking a hold. But what hold? Nowhere in it is the end, this transformation of the scene, nowhere in it is the thought that is free for itself so much as intimated. What is specific to the end has here no premises.

[[153]] Something essential is further added in the progression. From chemism, which is indeed taken in the wider sense altogether, the dialectic does not proceed immediately to the organism, as nature, say, translates the chemical processes in the lungs and in the stomach into organic ones. The intermediate link for the dialectic is teleology, the external end, which realises itself in an external material found already at hand. The life of nature has the end more inwardly within itself. The external end appears only in the free choice of man, and as it were only as the freest blossom that the inwardly purposive, organic life is able to bear. According to the course of development which we observe, ⟦61⟧ this external end, often a contingency of thought, is later than the internal one, in which freedom and necessity go together. Let us grasp the difficulty as it really is, without volatilising it into generality. How in all the world is the external, that is, the human end to form itself out of the negation of chemism? What a chasm lies between!

[[154]] And now the means of the external end. Whence is the power put into its hand, if now the subjective concept, drawn out of the object, having become free for itself, stands over against the object? "The concept is this immediate power, because it is the negativity identical with itself, in which the being of the object is determined throughout only as an ideal one." The concept has inherited the power of substance; since the objects are selfless over against substance and substance governs in them, the being of the object vanishes before the concept without resistance. Hence the end triumphs over things, so that they become its means. This seems consistent. But one thing has been overlooked, and indeed the most important. That concept which in this sense is called the negativity identical with itself, like substance itself, is the infinite concept. Against this, according to the course of the system, the being of the object can offer no resistance of any kind. But the end that is being spoken of is finite and external; and precisely in this limitation it must have forfeited that power. Hence the manner in which the possibility of realisation is ascribed to the finite end is only a semblance.

[[155]] Can the derivation then perhaps demonstrate how the infinite end (the idea) could realise itself? This development would have greater significance than that one; and though the right thing would not then have been accomplished in the right place, what is most essential would have been given in advance. To test this hope we must go back to the source of the plenitude of power which the concept has received. Everything ⟦62⟧ depends on how the concept has become the truth of substance.

[[156]] We consider this point in its naked simplicity.¹ Substance is not active against something, but only against itself as a simple element without resistance. In repelling itself into the accidents, it is causal. The substances arising thereby react against the first substance and act and react among themselves. In this reciprocal action the one is what the other is, cause and effect; they are identical. This pure alternation with itself is necessity unveiled. As substance runs its course through causality and reciprocal action, it becomes evident that independence is the infinite negative relation to itself, so that what is as independent and actual is only as the identity of substance. Through this reciprocal motion, which remains with itself, the truth of necessity is freedom and the truth of substance is the concept. In that substance remains with itself in the accidents, it is not blind but is the concept.

[[157]] Substance passes over into substances and therefore finds itself again in them. In reciprocal action the one and the other are cause, the one and the other effect. The one is what the other is. Substance therefore remains identical with itself. This remaining-with-itself is the concept.

[[158]] This derivation is merely formal; the content is set entirely aside. What the substances are, what the reciprocal actions produce, has no influence. This is the consolation of substance, that what comes out has again the form of substance and is just as much effect as cause; the one is what the other is. This formal, wholly external equalisation of reflection is the guarantee that substance ⟦63⟧ remains with itself; and from this stem freedom and the concept. But if the substances rebelled, if reciprocal action broke out into a war, that relation of like form, that grounding of identity with itself, would always be the same. Substance could even then still be content with itself; for even then substances would still arise in causality; even then, in action and counteraction, the one would be what the other is; both passive and active. But what a thin concept of being-with-itself, what a powerless freedom, what a contentless concept!

[[160]] > (2) Cf. above vol. I. p. 62ff.

[[161]] From this formal identity and from no other are freedom and the concept derived; for the sake of this identity the concept, like substance, is "the negativity identical with itself." The premises give nothing further. But from such a formal identity no power stems. In it the concept has, as it were, only the role of looking on, as substance, in the production of causality and in the action and reaction of reciprocal action, finds identical relations (forms of existence) again. Yet in the end there is need of the concept that predetermines and governs the content of existence. In that identity which has been demonstrated, the concept is neither conscious nor certain of the thing.

[[162]] Thus the concept as this "immediate power," against which the being of the object has no power, dissolves away when one leads the concept back to where it came from in the system.

[[163]] The treatment of the end has received a far-dazzling logical semblance from the fact that the end is traced back to the determinations of the syllogism. Subjectivity concludes itself together with objectivity in the terminus medius of the means. This side will be discussed further below in the doctrine of the syllogism.

[[164]] ⟦64⟧ Finally, the manner in which the finite end raises itself to the infinite end of the idea still demands a particular consideration. The end attained becomes means to another. The concepts of end and means become identical; the one is what the other is; and in this identity they exchange with one another into infinity. Hence the truth of the finite end is the idea, the absolute unity of the subjective and the objective. Two moments are active here: first that identity, in that the end attained offers itself as the material of another, so that the difference of end and means vanishes; for what was just now end now becomes means; then the course into infinity. That identity is no real one, only a logical one of reflection, not a pregnant one, as for example a seed, but a dull and flat one, like an external comparison. It says nothing at all; for in one relation something is end, in another means. End and means can become really identical only in the absolute whole. This concept is neither produced nor conditioned by that one. The progress into infinity, too, means little. For nowhere is there a direct compulsion to bend this advancing series of ends round into a circle, which is what would first matter. The alleged identity of end and means drives toward this just as little as the identity of the something and the other in their mutual relation drives from the so-called bad infinity to the positive infinity that returns into itself.¹

[[165]] Thus Hegel's derivation is at no point sufficient to develop the inner possibility of the concept of end and to ground the necessity of its dominion.

For those who declare the end to be merely subjective, a mere category of human thinking, there is, in face of the evident facts of the organic and the ethical, which compel us to comprehend them under the ⟦65⟧ end, only *one* way out. They must show that what appears to us as purposive stems in itself from the blindly efficient cause.

[[168]] The oldest attempt has been preserved for us by Aristotle¹ in some hints taken from Empedocles. If we bring it from its fragmentary presentation into a whole, it appears, with some inserted thoughts which we do not strictly pass off as Empedoclean, roughly as follows.

[[169]] In the strife of love and hate, of the combining and separating forces, elements and shapes meet. If they come together in such a way that, combined, they cannot maintain themselves, the formations perish in the moment in which they arose, as when, for example, a piece of a bull collided with a human face. But the elements and shapes which, meeting one another, so agree and so fit together that they can maintain themselves, remain; they have indeed come to be without an end, but once they have come to be they hold their ground, and in self-preservation they exhibit end and purposive activity. Because we men, we may add by way of supplement, know only of such formations as can maintain themselves—for the ill-fitting ones, which perished in arising, we do not know at all—we regard harmonious activity according to an internal end. The atomists, who explained the living and the lifeless from the shape, position and order of the atoms, must have proceeded similarly with the blind motion that brings the atoms together.

[[170]] What Aristotle objects against this view, in emphasising that the phenomena of nature are constant and always occur, but that the coincidence of chance can yield nothing lasting, does not suffice, because ⟦66⟧ the constant is provided for by the fact that only such formations remain as can maintain themselves, while all others have perished and do perish. To be sure, it will become harder and harder to attain the lasting through chance if that capacity for self-preservation, invented by chance, must extend so far as to explain the preservation of the kind as well, and thus, for example, propagation. Yet whoever once does not shrink from assuming that thousands upon thousands of formations perish before *one* remains (experience nowhere finds their remains and traces) has, in the hypothesis, gained the abiding and the lasting.

[[172]] Where chance, incalculable in the individual case, plays into causality, the human understanding calculates in general, as in dice or in card games, the probability that this or that combination will occur, and even expresses it in numerical ratios. With the growing number of elements which can unite, and unite each time in a different order, with the greater and yet bound composition that must be attained, with the more precise result that is in question, the probability of turning the end of nature into a play of nature sinks in a scarcely measurable, scarcely expressible ratio. Even the ancients, who did not know the rapidly and mightily rising ratios of the calculus of permutations and combinations, indicate what is right in a happy image. It is not probable, they say, that letters of all kinds thrown together and poured out, mingling as chance would have it, should compose a poem, so that in this way sense would come from the senseless. Perhaps it is still more difficult to assume that from the blind coincidence of chemical and ⟦67⟧ physical elements and forces any organ of the body, for example the bright, sharp, comprehensive eye, or even the concordant sum of the organs, the body as a whole, could spring, than that from letters blown together a book in which one might read thoughts should arise, as if by a chance that cancelled itself and turned into its very opposite. This path of haphazard gives us no hope of insight into how the seeing could arise from the blind, the precision of the organic, the persistence of what agrees, the satisfaction of life, and even self-conscious thought from the motley, confused jumble. The infinitely growing improbability is equivalent to impossibility.

[[177]] 9. In grounding the concept of end we distinguish the logical and the metaphysical side of the task. It is incumbent on us first to investigate how we know. If in doing so we must reach over into the nature of being in so far as the question is how thinking and being are mediated and how being in its actual nature is appropriated by thinking, then in the certainty that the end is there already lies a metaphysical knowledge, which would complete itself if it could be shown how the end comes to be in being.

[[178]] We continue first with the former.

[[179]] The position of motion and that of the end are essentially different. Motion, the original act of mind, was in general intelligible by itself; the end is not. It presupposes something to which it relates; at the least the shaping motion, as in mathematical problems, or the material world, which it animates with mind. Just as ⟦69⟧ the end in its execution is surrendered to external being, so it already arises in relation to that being and is therefore to be comprehended only in the midst of experience. In treating of the end, therefore, we have leapt over the whole physical world, which we receive as something given by virtue of the original motion, and we require these empirical elements as a condition.

[[180]] > (1) Phys. II. 8. middle p. 199 a 15, cf. polit. VII. 17. p. 1337 a 1.

[[181]] If we set thinking and being over against each other, then, presupposing the possibility of mediation, there results a twofold relation of cause. Either being acts on thinking, the thing on the concept, or thinking acts on being, the concept on the thing.

[[182]] We deliberately keep in view only the reciprocal relation of cause from thinking to being. Since we have shown a living intermediate link in motion, we are thereby entitled to speak of such an overreaching activity at all.

[[183]] The causality which keeps itself merely within the one or the other circle, in being or in thinking, we designate as the efficient cause. In thinking too? It may seem strange to extend the efficient cause to this domain of freedom. And yet it must be done, provided that the added condition is taken strictly. Where thought copies external things, it has received an excitation from the opposite circle and appropriated what is foreign. In such a case the physical nature of thinking cooperates, but already it no longer acts within itself and oversteps its sphere. But when thinking at first follows the projecting motion, or demands an accompanying image for the concept, or, as in the so-called association of ideas, determines the course of representations according to the sequence of time or the point of view of similarity, or, in the play of wit, lets the free reciprocal excitation of representations have its way, representations which there repel or attract one another as in chemical elective affinity: then we have ⟦70⟧ before us, as it were, the physiological nature of thinking, and we may here, although in a higher domain, speak of the efficient cause just as we determine, in the very midst of the service of the end, the efficient cause of the organs, for example the function of individual parts of the eye. We presuppose the efficient cause in this whole extent and ask further about the twofold relation designated above.

[[184]] If being acts on thinking, the fact on the process of understanding, there results in this relation the ground of knowing (causa cognoscendi). If thinking acts on being, the concept intervenes in the process of becoming, there results, on the contrary, the end (causa finalis).

[[185]] In the first case the effect of the real process becomes the cause of the logical one. For what is the later in being, thought produces the earlier; and it is at least the intention to undo the process of being in thinking and then to let the fact come to be once more, mentally, out of the producing ground. For example, the beautifully curved, mighty line and the wonderful play of colours of the suddenly rising rainbow strike the astonished mind. This appearance becomes an impulse to reflection. In the fact the understanding wishes to read the producing ground and then to project the appearance from the ground.

[[186]] In the second case the activity of the logical process becomes the cause of the real one. Thinking, already filled with the appearances, posits an effect and asks, so far as it has insight into the real process, how this is to be attained. The effect is what is willed, and for the sake of this effect the cause from which it proceeds is willed. This cause is only the secondary, but what is necessary through the end. Evidently two things work together here. First, what is has been transformed into thought, and thereby the ⟦71⟧ cause and effect of the real process have been known. Then, out of this thought and this knowledge, a power over the effect is acquired. Only in that a real organ which it governs is subject to thinking itself is thinking able, therefore, to intervene determiningly. For example, a tangent is to be drawn to a curve, say the circle. This problem contains the end and in it the end-point, the goal to be attained by a real activity. In so far as thinking possesses insight into geometrical formation and commands an organ that produces the figures, it can project and carry out the process that produces the tangent.

[[187]] In the first case the copying motion excited from without, in the second the prefiguring motion, is the active intermediate link. There the representation of the antecedent arises from the reality of the consequent, here from the representation of the consequent the reality of the antecedent and thereby likewise that of the consequent. There thought goes backward, here it reaches forward. There the ground of knowing (causa cognoscendi), here the knowledge of the ground is the living impulse.

[[188]] The possibility of this twofold reciprocal action between thinking and being lies always in the mediating motion. Hence it also happens that the efficient cause is intuited under the direction whence, the end under the direction whither (whereto).

[[189]] Just as we know external motion only through the mind's own motion, so we know the external end which nature has realised only because the mind itself projects ends and can therefore copy ends.

[[190]] When the end is worked out in the process that realises it and brought to knowledge as a free power, there shows itself in this the profundity of investigation, the transfiguration of the blind course of the cause. When the end ⟦72⟧ is set as a problem by the mind and this problem is happily solved, there shows itself in this the genius of invention. But the former stage is possible only through the latter.

[[191]] Thus the end intervenes in the sciences as a second a priori. Problems of mathematics, problems of mechanics and technology are free products of the mind hastening ahead of what is given. This a priori direction enters into the very midst of the empirical sciences. We may designate it only relatively as an a priori; for the elements with which the end operates are given to it; but out of them it creatively produces something new. Even in pure mathematics, where content and form are a priori, the end of the problem will presuppose elements as given, and it receives them no differently than when it otherwise receives them from experience.

[[192]] Because the mind in this way projects and carries out ends, it is able, going backwards, to understand those that have been projected and carried out. Let us now ask what compels it to leave the track of the efficient cause, which nevertheless offered itself to it as the first in the producing motion, and what vouches to it that the form of the end belongs not merely to its manner of consideration but to the thing itself.

[[193]] The question is similar to that at the beginning, when motion was posited as what is common to thinking and being; but it is more difficult. There everything pressed toward the assumption of motion, unless we wished, within us and outside us, to fall into the opposite, rest and death. Here it is not made so easy for us. The knowledge of the efficient cause is under way; it might even seem that we are being led into the arms of a dualism if we open a second path in the end.

[[194]] The necessity of giving up the efficient cause in its blind sole dominion, or rather of subordinating it to a higher ground, lies, however, in the impotence of the efficient cause itself. Where it suffices, we need no other ⟦73⟧ ground; and without its help the end is a phantom. But if, in face of phenomena such as those of organic life, explanation by the efficient cause founders, the mind must try another path. To be sure, at this standpoint the possibility still remains open that the efficient cause, more deeply investigated, may dissolve the view of the end into a semblance. Such an attempt must be expected. Until then, however, the incapacity of the efficient cause is the indirect proof of the necessity of the end. Light cannot be comprehended from darkness, and therefore we posit it as an activity of its own.

[[195]] But light reveals itself, and that is its proper proof. So too the end. When the continuity which is the essence of the efficient cause breaks off in time and space, when what has been interrupted restores itself to unity only in a higher thought, then this whole found again is the proper guarantee. Efficient activities that run apart, manifold directions that divide even to the point of opposition, now appear in surprising connection. They form a whole, just as they are determined by the whole. The thought of the whole is before the parts, the thought of the effect before the cause; this complete reciprocity between whole and parts has in itself a self-proclaiming clarity as soon as it is illuminated by the kindred mind.

[[196]] To every end there belongs a realising process, which consists in the concatenation of efficient causes. Now if there were a derivation from the efficient cause which ended with a formation of the end, for example man, this could be either the realisation of the willed end or else the development of a blind force, and the inference back would be doubtful. But such a situation will not easily occur. In order to solve the simplest geometrical problem, for example to draw a circle through three points which ⟦74⟧ do not lie in a straight line, we make several different starts. We draw straight lines from one point to the other as future chords, we erect perpendiculars from their midpoints, we take the radius from the point of intersection to one of the given points, and with it we describe the circle sought. On the side of the efficient cause there is here discontinuity; no continuation in the same direction of force; we start and break off and do so again. But the starts from different points are preformed in the end, the pervading thought of unity, and in this preforming thought a continuum is restored. In the steady development of the organic we do not see these breaks, which we perceive externally where we ourselves carry out the end; but we notice, in the differentiation through the germ, in the shaping and arrangement of the cells, in the different formation of the different members, the different directions laid down in advance.

[[197]] The end, indeed, as the invisible thought, cannot be observed like the external appearance; but it is nevertheless present in what can be observed, as the soul of the appearance. Itself a thought, it is accessible only to thought. But has it on that account less actuality? No grounding stands better. Within the efficient cause, too, the producing ground in its simplicity mostly lies beyond the motley, confused appearance, for example the generating cause of the wonderful world of colours beyond the rays that touch the eye. Just as in all such cases theory must try itself against the appearance until it covers it, just as it must agree with itself and enter again as a member into the concordant unity of the rest of knowledge, so the end has to fulfil the same conditions of a hypothesis. In this way it confirms itself in itself and in the system.

[[198]] ⟦75⟧ No strict criteria can be given, like an external measuring-rod. Since the end presupposes given elements and comes to execution only by means of the physical cause, it must, in order to be known, sustain a struggle with this cause. The broken thread of the efficient cause, the bold leap of the appearances, drives us at first to seek again the lost unity through the thought of the end; but the question arises ever anew: is the thread of physical activity really broken, or is the supposed leap of the appearances perhaps only a quicker step? The discontinuum is perhaps, more deeply investigated, a continuum, and the apparent continuum, on sharper consideration, divides into the members of the end. Because the sudden leap of the appearances urges us to leave the quiet course of the efficient cause, it happens that precisely chance, as in divination, counts as a sign of the end. In the old belief Iris, appearing as at a magic stroke, becomes the messenger of the gods, thus the bearer and herald of the end, until astonished wonder dissolves and freer consideration conjectures in her the mirror-image of the sun.

[[199]] We must not fail to recognise the share of motion in the thought of the end. If motion was the original activity of mind, it will be taken up into the intuition of the end.

[[200]] In the many-shaped, intertwined forms of motion we intuit efficient causes.¹ Where they subordinate themselves to the end, many are active together. The manifold play of combination which must be tried in order to find the significance of the individual causes for the end becomes possible only through freely projecting motion. The end thereby clothes itself in a peculiar intuition. The various forces working for *one* end (the ⟦76⟧ efficient causes) must incline together toward *one* point and point toward it in their direction. This point, in many cases only ideal, but intimated and necessarily posited by the course and order of the forces, designates for intuition the unity of the ends in the fullness of the serving forces. This convergence of directions accompanies the end in such a way that where it cannot be demonstrated in the appearance, the end too is not to be recognised. The divergence of directions, which, followed out absolutely, leads to complete dissolution, scatters the forces which the end has to gather, and is in the appearances the sign that they withdraw from the dominion of a higher unity.

[[202]] If in this way the intuition of motion, in determining itself more precisely, takes the end up into itself, then the categories projected from motion will also appropriate the end and thereby pass over into denser shapes of the concept. We shall therefore attempt later to present how these categories develop through the end.

[[203]] 10. Were the consideration of ends to complete itself, one thing more would have to be added from the metaphysical side.

[[204]] We have attempted to show that the end is actual in nature and becomes knowable, or, what after the preceding considerations is the same, a thought at the ground of things, which directs and guides the forces.

[[205]] At the beginning of our investigations it could not be asked how motion comes to be in being; for that already required motion. With the end, which presupposes efficient causes as its means, it is otherwise, and the question how the end comes to be in being at all has its right. For this, so far, only the ideal premises have been known, above all that anticipation of the whole before the parts, of the effect before the cause, possible only through thought, and that consistency in the demand for means, possible only through thought. But knowledge of the real ⟦77⟧ side has lagged behind. Nowhere in nature do we observe the point at which thought takes hold of and seizes force and leads it toward its ends, and speculation is nowhere able to show it. The consideration that seeks the internal end grounds the ideal in the real; but it still lacks knowledge of how the ideal comes into the real, enters into the real. Just as the ancients represented Helios standing boldly on his chariot, guiding the horses of the sun with his hand, but gave the hand no reins, the instruments of human tractive force, so the thought of the end governs the efficient forces with invisible reins. The human thought of the end disposes of the executing hand, and the hand initiates that real process which corresponds to the consistent projection of the means. For the process in nature the correspondence breaks off at this place, and it is chiefly into this gap in knowledge that the doubt throws itself which regards the end with disbelief. It is not impossible that our knowledge may one day be completed. For now let it suffice to know what we know and what we do not know.

[[206]] We remarked at the beginning that all knowledge rests on a community of thinking and being, and in agreement with this we have found in the end that our thought, which projects ends, understands the ends realised in being. Here too we must not leave a doubt unmentioned.

[[207]] "A concept at the ground of things," it is asked, "similar to ours?" Our concept, it is asserted, is only a certain "motion or affection of the pulpy mass in the skull," our concept is mediated through and through, and this is supposed to become equal to the original divine one?

[[208]] We disregard the crude conception which calls the mysterious, probably most profound organ a pulpy mass because it is still not understood, and puts thought in the shade because thought dwells in it. It is to be ⟦78⟧ wondered at how the original motion, which no adherent of blindly efficient causes denies, becomes so free and conscious in the human mind that with it the mind copies and appropriates external motion and creates geometry; and yet it happens. The agreement is likewise to be wondered at when the composite organisation helps the simple principle at the ground of things to be known and human thought to reach the thought in being; and yet it happens. In art, in experiment, in practice this agreement confirms itself, in that things answer harmoniously to the deed which accords with the end that has been grasped. We wonder at this agreement, which is the highest achievement of the internal end, but after our investigations we cannot doubt it.

[[209]] 11. A further remark offers itself here, which is perhaps not unimportant for psychological development. It has already been pointed out in passing¹ that the organs of motion act in the most intimate agreement with sight. When the eye strives into the distance, that is an ideal motion, while the bending and stretching of the joints, walking and grasping, actually traverse space and can therefore be designated a real motion. Sight directs the organs of motion, and these carry out the direction. The ideal motion here reaches over the real, the directing over the producing and advancing. The one motion is taken up into the other, and here, as it were externally, in the schema of motion, the dominion of the end over the efficient cause presents itself. This intuition runs like a guiding image through the whole domain of the end and is still recognisable even in the most spiritual heightening of intention.

[[211]] ⟦79⟧ The following detailed note is intended to enter into a theory of the present day, which seeks to remove the end from the consideration of nature, so far as it concerns the validity of this concept. For since in the foregoing the end has not been constructed in some a priori way, but sought out in the actual and its knowability then demonstrated, the investigation must ask whether this basis in organic life has in fact been withdrawn from it.

[[212]] Note. 1. The theory of the end presented here may well be called, as has been done, the anthropomorphic one; for it can make the end, which runs through nature as a fact, comprehensible only in the way in which man exercises it, through thought and will at the ground of things. No other way of comprehending it has yet been found.

[[213]] The inference that something is true because it is anthropomorphic opens the door to every deception, every semblance; it is the fallacy of the thoughtless crowd and of our impetuous affects. The inference, on the other hand, that something is untrue because it is anthropomorphic bars all access to knowledge; for foreign things disclose themselves to thinking only in the activities which we consciously exercise and which at the same time, even if blindly, underlie things; it matters only that they prove themselves as such. In this way mathematics, springing from the constructive motion of our mind, thus subjectively (anthropomorphically), penetrated into material things and their forces; and in the same way the end, a fundamental concept of human reason, becomes comprehensible in things. If the former is not doubted in its results, it gives the latter, for its part, a share in its certainty.

[[214]] The more recent natural sciences in particular make war on the anthropomorphic end.

[[215]] 2. Since Darwin wrote his book on the origin of species by means of natural selection, or the preservation of favoured races in the struggle for life (1859), since this book of luminous empirical research drove on to its metaphysical consequences in Germany, the victory of efficient causes has been celebrated and the concept of end removed from the world or left to human poetic invention.

[[216]] This means more than when Spinoza declared the concept of end to be a human invention and yet afterwards took it up again as an auxiliary concept. It means more because, instead of the abstract generality which Spinoza pronounced, today the full and entire labour of the natural sciences, labour in the almost ⟦80⟧ immeasurable domain of the concrete, has been summoned to make the proposition true.

[[217]] It is a new piece of German philosophy of nature, and indeed distinguished from the former one in that it does not construct from generalities, but demonstrates the ground plan in the given material and keeps itself open to every correction by what is given. Such earnest work on the genesis of organic life, on the family tree of beings, will be mistaken in its intention only by one who does not know the need of research for unity and development. It is the business of the natural scientists to exercise criticism in particulars and to test the hypothesis of the principle. In investigations which turn on the value of the concept of end, it cannot be avoided to examine the new view with regard to this fundamental concept. It is incumbent on them to guard a concept which, if mistaken in nature, would also be mistaken in the ethical. It is a fact in the history of philosophy, attested by Socrates and Plato and Aristotle, that with the concept of the end in nature the inner vocation of man was more deeply known, and the concepts of the organic and the ethical mutually deepened and illuminated one another. In what follows we leave the hypothesis standing as such and ask only how the concept of end stands in relation to it.

[[218]] 3. We must first recall the main features of the theory.

[[219]] Deeply grasped analogies have not seldom guided inventive science, and a thoroughly carried-out twofold analogy forms the essence of Darwin's doctrine. The one is the analogy of the origin of varieties for the origin of species in the vegetable and animal kingdoms, the analogy of artificial selection for the breeding of new varieties for the assumption of a natural breeding through selection for the production of new species. The other is the analogy of competition in the market of life, which awakens and heightens forces, or of the wars for power in history, for the animals struggling with one another for the necessities of life, which in this struggle test their forces, increase them and acquire new ones.

[[220]] The gardener who aims at a particular colour of blossom in a species of flower selects the seeds of those individual plants which tend toward this colour, for example, among the white-blossoming ones, those that are turning reddish. He sows the seed of these plants in isolation and proceeds with the seed of these plants again according to the same point of view of selection. The selection of seeds from plants blossoming redder and redder leads, through repetition, to the goal. A variety with red blossoms arises. Pigeon breeding, whose practice goes back to ancient Egypt, ⟦81⟧ has, in the long time of its existence, through artificial selection in pairing following an intention, produced varieties which differ so essentially in their habits, performances, their ornament, in the transformation of their structure and even in their anatomical characteristics, that they could pass for different species; and yet it is shown that they all descend from a single wild parent species, the blue rock pigeon. The races of dogs are another example. Sheep breeding and horse breeding succeed in a similar way in attaining and fixing particular properties through selection in breeding. Suitable conditions of life, which are supplied to plants or animals through cultivation, for example to plants a soil mixed with the right substances, to animals better nourishment, assist breeding. In these examples, which extend as far as the human hand tends plants and animals, it is shown what continued artificial breeding is able to accomplish through inheritance in producing varieties which can grow so in their differences that they come to equal species.

[[221]] The question now is how a similar process could form itself in nature. In place of the plan of men another impulse enters, the struggle for life, the struggle for the necessary conditions of existence, which creatures wage among themselves. Plants contend with one another for the space of soil which they need for their roots, for sunlight and moisture, without which they do not thrive. Animals contend with their enemies, who seek them as food, with beasts of prey and with parasites; they contend with their own kind living in the same place for the means of life; they contend against hostile influences of every sort. The stronger or craftier conquer; the others succumb, are displaced and themselves perish. Germs of life produced a thousandfold, blossoms, seeds, eggs, the young of animals, animals as prey for food, perish, and the life laid down miscarries in this war of all against all before even *one* of these germs of life, favoured by circumstances in the struggle for existence, attains full development. In this struggle for life the victorious beings develop their forces in the direction that is useful to them; they cultivate their strength and pass it on with habit to their descendants. We take up an example which is offered to us. Where plants struggle with lack of water, those individuals which have hairy leaves have an advantage; for they are able to draw moisture from the air to themselves. As the plants with barer leaves perish, the hairy ones pass on their property and increase it in a natural drive. As the hairiness increases and ⟦82⟧ therefore draws the sap to itself and takes it away from other parts, the plant is transformed in other respects too. Reciprocal action with the whole environment, in this struggle for life, stamps shapes and forms upon creatures. It stimulates the forces, in that it combats or favours them, and through inheritance, through the series of generations in which the differences grow, it gradually forms constant species.

[[222]] In this process two things work together: adaptation and inheritance.

[[223]] Adaptation to the conditions of life, which every organism practises, the self-activity with which it wins from the external what it needs for its existence, entails alterations of its shape, of the structure of its organs, which become permanent through inheritance. Thus, for example, a gilled salamander from Mexico, which lives and reproduces in water with its water-breathing organs, was observed in the Paris Jardin des Plantes. A number of the animals crept out of the water onto land; they lost their gills and were now transformed into a gill-less salamander form that breathes through lungs. Conversely, parasites, originally independent organisms, feeding on foreign force upon the ground of foreign life, lose the organs they no longer use. Through this adaptation to the condition of life creatures become more manifold and perfect and heighten themselves in their formation. Even composite organs of the utmost perfection, such as the eyes, have on this view developed in the struggle for life, through adaptation to the elements, from the first eye-spots of the lowest beings. The psychical is connected with this. Through taming, which compelled animals to adapt to the house, domestic animals such as the dog have been weaned of wildness. The division of labour, which shows itself in the self-preservation of the individual where the organs develop out of the general life for particular functions, for example those of the senses, throws itself outward in animals when, as with the bees, the ants, the swimming colonies of hydromedusae, animal states form, in which the individuals unite for the struggle for existence and build and form themselves differently according to the determination of their functions.

[[224]] Natural selection works through adaptation and heredity; it works through the strength and excellence of the being toward a higher strength and excellence of the same.

[[225]] More recent geology intervenes in this explanation of the species forming themselves in the struggle for existence. In investigating the earth's crust, it acquaints us with an unmeasured past in the formation of the ground on which the world of the organic ⟦83⟧ is built up, as, for example, in the basin of New Orleans a series of buried cypress forests has been found one above another as lignite beds, separated by intermediate layers, and in individual forests of these strata tree trunks with five thousand annual rings. In the main geological periods, which some would reckon by millions of millennia, the successive generations, which continued themselves even through the divisions of the periods, had sufficient time, in the struggle for life, to lay down and carry out the division of labour, and to add up from generation to generation the differences they had acquired and to fix them through habituation and inheritance. Nature has time to accomplish its breeding through selection, which needs favourable chance. On this view each formation designates not a new and complete act of creation, but only a scene, for the most part torn out quite at random, from a slowly proceeding drama; for the intermediate forms which could represent the continuum have perished in the same struggle for existence, and we know only what was able to preserve itself.

[[226]] The comparative history of development of living beings in the embryonic states serves to confirm descent. For the egg of the higher animal, at first not different from the egg of the lower, passes from stage to stage through the forms which are proper to the embryo in the series of preceding creatures, as, for example, the embryo of a tortoise in the sixth week is compared with the embryo of a chicken on the eighth day after conception. It is assumed that the development of species and genera, which required many millennia, is compressed into short periods in the development of the embryo, for example of man.

[[227]] This theory led its originator to a few first created species, which, developing, underlie the world of life in its innumerable forms.

[[228]] The drive toward unity led further in Germany. The mechanical origin of all creatures from matter, which is eternal, is supposed to be the necessary consequence, and *one* spontaneous generation by physical or chemical means the necessary presupposition. No end intervenes in the whole process of origination. Everything is the work of efficient causes. The family tree of the creature, which branches out, leads from the monera, the lowest microscopic beings, solely through development in the struggle for existence up to the mammals, and from the lowest mammals through the apes to man in *one* uninterrupted line. This natural system represents the descent that actually took place, not ⟦84⟧ merely a kinship in concept but a kinship in flesh and blood. Nature had time, but it is itself only *one*, the nature of the causes efficient in matter. Every other view of it is dualistic; the end, a transcendental formation, founds this discord. The monism of matter is victor and a new epoch of mankind begins.¹

[[229]] With this claim the theory works among us today.
