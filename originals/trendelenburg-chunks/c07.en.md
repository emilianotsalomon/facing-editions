[[404]] # ⟦142⟧ XI. THE REAL CATEGORIES FROM THE END.

[[405]] 1. The categories derived above are the universal forms of concepts, in so far as motion underlies thinking and being in the same way.

[[406]] Through their origin they are necessary, but through the immeasurable possibility of the act that produces them they are of the widest extent. They are able to take experience up into themselves, because experience, as has been shown, rests on the condition of motion. On this path they limit themselves in particulars and grow together with new determinations, without giving up the first and universal foundation. Those categories therefore run like the warp-threads through the densest and richest web of our representations and form the real support of the fabric.

[[407]] The fundamental concepts thus gained are now more closely determined by the end, as the universal is by a specific difference. Like them, the end springs from the mental world and is found again in the bodily. It has already been indicated above how it fuses with the intuition of motion. How, then, do those concepts take shape when ⟦143⟧ the end pervades them and gathers their elements around a new centre?

[[408]] 2. What the end projects, as the form, what it demands for its realisation, as the matter, what it directs, as the efficient cause, we call in the wider sense its means. But in the narrower sense the efficient cause, serving the end, is called means. It is so in a pre-eminent way, since motion also conditions matter and form. Regarded from the task of the end, the means is something demanded and in so far an end subordinate to the first end.¹ In so far as the activity of the end fixes itself in an efficient cause and appropriates and possesses it, the end itself appears as physical cause. The organ must accomplish its end. It is, for example, the law of the eye that it see, in the same sense as it is (within the efficient cause) a law of the mirror that it reflect the ray of light. Only penetrating thought recognises the difference. The means is degraded to a mere efficient cause when the activity is indeed accomplished but the end is not attained. When, for example, the eye stares blankly into the world, when the open ear lets the sounds glide past, when the thoughts dream while awake: then the purposive organ sinks to a merely physical potency, the meaningful means to a blind cause.

[[409]] 3. The substance of the efficient cause was understood as a whole closed in itself, and indeed through the reproduction of the peculiar law of origin that underlies it. If now this law of formation is determined by the end, there results from the universal concept of substance either the concept of the machine or that of the organism.

[[410]] In the machine (the mechanism) the end is at work, but as one given from outside. Material, form and moving ⟦144⟧ cause are brought together in the machine like three different things. They are indeed destined for one another; but the end that determines them is to them an alien power, an external constraint. According to the end the material is chosen, the form projected, the motion imparted. The parts exist for themselves; the whole is put together out of the parts. Only in the hand of the alien understanding does it fulfil its destination. Here too the whole is thought before the parts, but the parts do not first come to be in the whole. Everything stands externally over against everything else, and only the alien intelligence cancels this external relation, so that the thought may realise itself in the activity.

[[411]] > (1) Aristot. phys. II. 3. p. 194a 3.

[[412]] In the organism material, form, moving cause and end exist, as it were, with one another and through one another. The end, as the indwelling principle, builds the body. The material is appropriated so peculiarly that even chemically organic matter bears its specific character. The form is not impressed upon the material from outside but produced from within. The moving cause is not imparted, but is so governed by the end that it becomes formative force. Each part is there through all the others just as much as it comes into being for the sake of the others and of the whole. The parts come to be through the whole and maintain themselves only in the whole; detached, they lose their subsistence together with the end. The unity is a unity of development, which proceeds from the whole, not of composition, which arises from the parts.

[[413]] It can and must indeed be said that the end is given to individual organisms, and that they do not create it out of themselves. Were the latter the case, their freedom would be complete. But the given end becomes the property of the organism and is active in it from within.

[[414]] Mechanism and organism have the end in common, but there it remains alien property, here it becomes their own life.

[[415]] ⟦145⟧ When Leibniz still determined the organic body as forming a machine not merely as a whole but also in its smallest parts,¹ he probably had before his eyes, on the one side, the end observed down to the smallest folds of the organism, and on the other his assumption of individual monads. Kant cleared up the difference between the two concepts, and on this point one can only ever refer to him.²

[[416]] Meanwhile a confusion has crept into usage. Kant speaks of the mechanism of nature precisely where all ends are denied and the phenomena are explained only from the efficient cause. We likewise speak of mechanical habit, of the mechanism of method, of mechanical memory-work and so on, thinking thereby only of the pressure and impact of driving causes, or, what is the same, of the blind force of the time-series. The name is too good for the thing. In it only *one* side of the machine, thoughtless force, is considered, but not also the other, the mental end.

[[417]] 4. The closer determinations of the remaining categories already lie in the intuition, just designated, of mechanism and organism as governed by the end.

[[418]] Above, in accordance with the principle, unity in plurality was conceived as fixed motion. From generating motion plurality necessarily flowed; from the possibility of the motion that really holds together and logically comprehends sprang the unity that reaches over plurality. The contradiction of unity and plurality that arises for the dissecting understanding was traced back to this simple ⟦146⟧ intuition and seemed, within the efficient cause, to resolve itself in the fundamental requirement of the investigation, the continuum of motion.

[[421]] The logical unity of plurality reproduced the real, and the real rested in the last resort on the constant law of formation. Within the efficient cause, by the nature of progressive motion, plurality was what came first, and the task was to show how this plurality gathers itself into unity. This happened through the same principle, but through a counter-motion. Within the efficient end, on the other hand, the relation is reversed. The thought, hence the unity compressed into a living point, is what comes first, and the end realises itself only in that the thought subjects external activities to itself and determines the law of formation of the thing. This plurality in unity is here the task, as there unity in plurality.

[[422]] In the domain of the efficient cause unity arises through the continuum of motion, through attraction directed toward a common point, but always through a blind force, and logical unity derives from the real unity thus prefigured. In the realised end the relation is reversed. Unity is originally unity of thought, and the understanding has only to find it again when it has presented itself externally.

[[423]] Plurality within the efficient cause needed unity if it was not to scatter into the infinite. The possibility of this unity came from the counter-motion. In the end, conversely, the unity of thought, if it is not to vanish like a shadow of representation, needs things and activities. The possibility of this process is conditioned by the motion common to thinking and being and by the mastery over experience acquired by means of it.

[[424]] ⟦147⟧ This unity of the end appears in mechanism and in the organism, though in different ways, as has already been indicated. Thus the old problem of unity in plurality is resolved, in the domain of the end, by thought itself, and organic unity is its highest presentation.

[[425]] Examples show the gradation. Let one compare, say, within the efficient cause, the unity that binds the stone or the activities of a neutral product with the unity that guides from outside the construction and the motions of a machine, and with the unity that pervades the various functions of a living organ, for example the eye, toward the attainment of its end.

[[426]] 5. With unity the concept of whole and parts receives a new meaning. Even in the machine the parts no longer behave indifferently toward one another; through the end they are mutually demanded. In the organism the parts, which appeared externally in the whole, become members, which the life of the individual brings forth and which in turn bring forth life. The thought of the whole determines the functions of the members, and the members serve the realisation of the whole. The rigid representation of the part is heightened to the mental concept of the member, that is, of the part that accomplishes a peculiar end. The parts are enclosed by the whole, the members pervaded by the life of the whole.

[[427]] Through this relation inherence has become more intimate. If it was asserted above that the change of inhering accidents, as indifferent toward the persisting substance, does not follow from the intuition of the efficient cause, and that such a representation becomes linked only afterwards with the relation of inherence: then there now ⟦148⟧ becomes clear here the differing significance of the parts. The essence lies in the end of the whole; and it strives for its realisation, as it were, in different gradations of the parts. Those members, or members of members, without which the end of the whole comes to nothing, are one with it, while others, standing in a more remote connection, can change without destroying the whole. We measure this significance of the activities, and as it were the degrees of the essential, by the necessary demands that the end of the whole makes if it is to fulfil or maintain itself at all. The wide names of substance and accident are for the most part tacitly filled by the thought of the end.

[[429]] The members, through the peculiar, if subordinate, end that they accomplish, receive a centre of their own and a particular life, which is indeed rooted in the life of the enclosing whole but comes forth in a certain independence.

[[430]] In this way the articulating end has a twofold activity, in that it raises the particular part into the life of the universal just as much as it stamps the universal whole into the particular life of the members. Thus the end works, to recall old problems by the name, at once generalising and individualising.

[[431]] 6. Within the efficient cause reciprocal relation was nothing but the unity of parts and properties, or the counterplay of forces that meet. Through the end reciprocal action receives a higher meaning. Since in the organic whole the members are, toward one another and toward the whole, reciprocally end and means, cause and effect, organisation has rightly been called by Schelling a higher potency of the category of reciprocal action. The reciprocal relation of the efficient cause is a mutual play of blind forces; organic reciprocal action has the finest bond, thought as master of the ⟦149⟧ forces. In organic reciprocal action the reciprocal relation of the efficient cause is wholly contained, the inner interpenetration of the parts into the whole, of the properties into the thing. This foundation has remained with the same necessity with which the end accomplishes itself only through the efficient cause. But the first concept of reciprocal action is profoundly developed, in that it takes up into itself the reciprocal action of thought, and now part against part and part against whole present a twofold, intimately fused reciprocal relation, a reciprocal action as much logical as physical. In the reciprocal relation of the efficient cause the reproduction by supervening thought is a contingent addition; in organic reciprocal action the thought that has become one with the physical cause is the innermost nature of the thing.

[[432]] > (1) Schelling transscendentaler Idealismus. 1800. p. 254: "Succession returning into itself, presented in rest, is organisation."

[[433]] When organisms which are independent by themselves, or are thought as independent, enter into an organic reciprocal action by realising a new end together, this higher unity is usually called a system. Thus one speaks of the solar system, or in organic geography of the system of a mountain range, and so on. The word, which otherwise in our language expresses a logical organisation, receives the sense of a real one, just as conversely the organic is transferred from the realm of the bodily world to the mode of knowledge.

[[434]] 7. If, proceeding from motion, quality was determined as the efficient cause that adheres to the substance, then this concept is shaped and stamped by the end into organic activity.

[[435]] The old content remains, but it is, as it were, reborn through a mental meaning. The cause proceeds from the substance, but is determined by its end. How this is to be understood will readily become clear from examples. We say, for instance: the eye sees, the crystalline lens refracts the ray of light, and thereby we express the quality of the eye, of the ⟦150⟧ lens. The relation, taken in the wider sense, is no different from when, say, within the efficient cause, attraction and repulsion under the law of polarity and so on are stated as the quality of the magnet. But those organic activities stand in the service of the end. The seeing eye is the light of the body; the refracting lens is the sense of the eye that gathers the rays out of their dispersion.

[[436]] Organic activities do not merely stream out from the life of the whole, as within the efficient cause the qualitative activities do from the substance, but they also return into it, in that they take place for the whole just as much as they are performed by the whole. If within the efficient cause the manifestation of properties recoils upon the thing, this is not the destination of the property, but an alien reaction or its own incapacity. In organic activity this return is the innermost essence.

[[437]] Within the moving cause the qualitative activities are blind forces that have no other measure than their effect. Their might is their right. Organic activity has, through the end whose instrument it is, a judge. The end that is to be attained is the norm that passes judgment on organic activity, as to whether it is sufficient or deficient. Organic activity ought to correspond to the end; and the question presses itself upon us of its own accord whether the activity is adequate to the end or not. Thus negation, until then a mere limit, receives the meaning of (qualitative) lack, of privation.

[[438]] Where Aristotle takes the concept of privation (of lack) ⟦151⟧ in the narrower and proper sense, he takes as its basis the determination of nature as measure,¹ and the Stoics apply it in the same way.² In this meaning it passes into the more recent logic, as for example into Melanchthon, and Leibniz uses it in the Theodicy in this sense. Kant is the first to turn the traditional usage into an alien relation.³ Following Aristotle's example, blind, predicated of a man, is privation at the organic level; deluded would be such at the ethical. Systems that lack the end have no measure for privation and do not know the concept in its proper sense. Spinoza (Letter 34) declares privation (lack) to be a mere thing of thought, a representation of the imagination, which expresses no essence and arises only from our comparing things with one another or with an earlier state of themselves. The blind man whom we see before us is as little deprived of sight as the stone that does not see; that is, according to the order of nature, seeing now belongs as little to the nature of this man as it belongs to the nature of a stone. In the same sense Schelling, following Spinoza, removes the concept of privation, as a product of mere imagining, from the realm of rational knowledge.⁴ In the series of efficient causes, to be sure, the arrest of development which produced the blindness was necessary; but the mind, which over against this necessity considers the inner destination, forms just as necessarily the category of lack; and guided by it, it strives to remove the lack, hence ⟦152⟧ to heal the blind man, to free the deluded man from his delusion. In the objective system of fundamental concepts the concept of lack springs up together with the standard of the inner end; the more liberal language forms it earlier; for even the expectation that is not satisfied by a thing ascribes to the thing the lack that properly lies in the expecting representation. Thus one may say that a line which is presupposed to be equal to another lacks equality. Similarly it happens that comparing thought, from a higher standpoint, imputes to an object an alien excellence which it cannot have, for example when one says that the stone lacks life. But the proper sphere of the fundamental concept, which must follow from the essence of the thing, is not determined by such reflections.

[[444]] 8. It has further been shown above that the concept of force enters only at the moment of reciprocal action, namely where a new unity forms between two or more elements; and within efficient causes we rejected in it the representation of a tendency or striving that would be innate as force in the individual thing. Where the end determines the concept of force, that foundation remains; but the latter representation, which we excluded there, gains a certain truth; for through the end force is destined and disposed for future reciprocal action, in the mechanical for an alien thought, in the organic for its own essence and life. In ⟦153⟧ mechanism, for example, the force of the knife to cut demands reciprocal action with the force of the resisting hard body; but the edge is preformed for the division of what is hard. In the organic, force is there only in reciprocal action, for example of the individual with the condition of life. The force of the eye to see is there together with the force of light to make visible; but in the eye that activity is disposed for reciprocal action, and the eye attains its inner end only in it; hence the eye longs, as it were, for stimulation by light. In this sense one can speak of a tendency, an appetitus naturae. Thus organic force is force disposed, for the life of the whole, toward a determinate reciprocal action.

[[447]] 9. Quantity was derived above as extensive and intensive, as continuous and discrete magnitude. It emerged as the blind product of motion, and the differences that were found derived solely from motion. Hence it came about that until then, as became evident in particular from the example of geometrical similarity, quantity appeared indifferent toward quality. The same figure of the triangle (its qualitative law) could clothe itself in infinitely different magnitudes. Now the end enters, and quantity, extensive and intensive, is bound; and the phenomenon is completed only when quantity is so adequate to the end that nothing can be taken away and nothing added without disturbing the harmony. Excess and deficiency, plus and minus, are determined according to the end. The negative therefore appears here analogously to the way it does in quality. If above quantity might show itself as the external and therefore indifferent element, it now serves the actuality of the concept and is raised by it and by the organic activities to the symmetry of the whole.

[[448]] Hegel¹ found the essence of quantity in the fact ⟦154⟧ that "determinateness is posited no longer as one with pure being, but as sublated or indifferent." Now since the truth of each determination is supposed to be preserved at the higher stage as a moment, while it perishes as a pretended totality, this concept of quantity too would have to continue through the further shapes. But this is not so. Quantity determined by the end is the opposite of that definition, which holds only within the efficient cause. It follows, then, that that determination contains not the original essence but only a one-sided observation. In organic magnitude the essence of magnitude cannot have perished in the way the given determination wholly perishes. Quantity rests in the intuition of space and number produced by motion; and this intuition may take new determinations up into itself, it always remains in its essence.¹

[[450]] With the concept of intensity the case is similar. It has been shown above that a pervasive intuition resting on motion underlies it. Where an inverse relation obtains between the two factors of motion, where in a shorter time a greater space, or in a longer time a smaller space, is traversed, or where in the real a relation analogous to this intuition appears: there the concept of (greater or lesser) intensity prevails. Here too the end binds what at first appeared unbound. In accordance with experience there usually forms a maximum and minimum of intensity which the end tolerates, and a middle relation by which, as the normal, intensity is measured. What lies below the minimum and above the maximum appears monstrous. According to the manifold ends experience alone decides here, and it remains a task of empirical research to fathom in detail the connection between the magnitudes ⟦155⟧ of the phenomenon and the end that determines the whole. Here it can only be indicated how, in relations of magnitude too, the end works from *one* sense and leads all elements to a concordant phenomenon.

[[452]] 10. The mathematical categories of position and sequence (the spatial location and succession of the elements) were brought out above.¹ They remain in the mechanical and the organic; but their meaning grows when they are determined and bound by the end. Then there springs from them the concept of order or arrangement.

[[453]] 11. If a number was generated through the comparison of two homogeneous magnitudes, there resulted in this the measure in the mathematical sense. The determination of magnitude through the end of the thing is measure in the ideal sense.²

[[454]] The former is brought to the thing externally, the latter lies in its essence. The former derives from an alien calculation, the latter from the reason of the thing. There the substrate of quantity is what comes first, here the norm of the end (something qualitative).

[[455]] Since the end always presupposes a manifold whose members are related to one another, measure, in its fully carried-out dominion, becomes proportionate symmetry. Phenomenon and thought here mutually enhance each other. The measure of the individual that accords with the whole is nothing other than the beautiful appearance of the concept of ends in its magnificent harmony. Herein lies the pleasure of intuition and the joy of thought, since nowhere do they excite each other in so even an interplay.

[[458]] ⟦156⟧ The transition from purely mathematical measure to measure determined by the end can be observed in geometry itself. The magnitude of figures is, as was shown, indifferent toward the shaping law. But in the analytical problem, which becomes a problem through the end, a given magnitude entails the determination of the others, if the requirement is to be satisfied. This example is the simplest phenomenon of measure determined by the end. With richer elements the significance grows. The plastic work of art shows measure in its purest perfection; and on the ethical domain prudent measure transfigures the action of man, since in it the inner and outer elements are balanced with mental force. Plato, who regards the world, the work of divine art, with the Greek eye of the sculptor, raised measure in this ideal sense to the essence of his philosophical view. When, in opposition to the sophistic saying that sets up man as the measure of the world, he calls God the measure of all things, the depth of the expression, in the Platonic sense, discloses itself only when the end, which rests in God, the Good, is fully recognised as the ruler of the world's formation.¹

[[459]] 12. Inner and outer become a peculiar category only through the end. Within the efficient cause this opposition is referred only to our sense. What is hidden from it is called an inner, although in itself it is just as much an outer. In sound, say, the wave-motion of the air is called the inner of the thing; but this motion is itself an outer, since after all it appears. One speaks of the inner of a disease when it has its seat in an enclosed organ of the body; but this inner is in itself something outer and spatial. Only with the end does the inner gain a more significant sense, even if the ⟦157⟧ name does not quite correspond. The inner of the thing now designates the end before its realisation, that which is yet to come to be.

[[461]] 13. It has been shown above that we receive the representation of matter, not form it, and that, however far motion may penetrate, a last point remains uncomprehended, in which an identity of being and activity must be presupposed. If matter manifests itself first of all in resistance, it remains true to its nature in also resisting a priori speculation and revealing itself as limitation. Where thinking and being are distinguished, matter is tacitly understood along with being, as the substrate. If one starts from being, matter is what is first and powerful. If one starts from the end, it appears as what is second and serving. Here it is the necessary as what is demanded, there as what rules and demands.

[[462]] Mere thought is indeed a living point, but solitary and without contact; the end already strives beyond it into the expanse and creates for itself a bodily existence only in matter. Without separating, supporting matter there would be no hold for thought and no individual life at all.

[[463]] Since the end always wills an activity, for what is absolutely at rest appears dead and worthless, and since this activity manifests itself in a bodily or mental motion, while the mental in turn subsists only in so far as it adheres to and has a hold in individual life, there appear in matter, in so far as it serves the end, two opposites, firmness and mobility. The end needs both, although they contradict each other; and it labours to reconcile them for its demands.

[[464]] As the end in general relates only to given elements ⟦158⟧ and is known only in experience, so here the inexhaustible wealth of material existence enters, and the infinite manifoldness of physics and chemistry. In general nothing can be determined here except that matter becomes means. In that mind enters appreciatively into the nature of matter, it "persuades" it to take the end up into itself and to be transfigured by thought. That in living nature organic matter also has a peculiar character of chemical compounds is a significant result of recent natural science.

[[465]] > (1) See vol. I. p. 254 ff.

[[466]] The thought of the end in its ideal greatness and given matter in its compelling necessity stand over against each other; and the means always falls short of the perfection of the thought, and the bold idea must pass through the means before it reaches its goal, and even in its victory it is curbed and tamed by the material. As the scientific thought of the natural scientist must first pass through the instrument of observation and suffers many an injury on this path, so the creative end suffers in the material, despite the service it renders as the condition of all life; it is always still an inadequate expression of the thought; there always remains a rigid, limiting residue which does not resolve into thought, and from which every finite realisation of the end is threatened with destruction. Thus the positive and negative side that comes forth in the relation of material to end may be designated in general.

[[467]] 14. The form deriving from the efficient cause is the bare figure in the mathematical sense; the form determined by the end, the stamp of the organ, is, taking the word in the wider sense, the articulated form. Mathematical and organic form meet in the regular and symmetrical shape, which can spring forth from the rhythm of moving causes just as it is projected from the ⟦159⟧ end of thought. Symmetry and regularity in form are the highest product of efficient force and become in turn, in many cases, a task of the end.

[[468]] Since organic form is the outermost appearance of the end, it is to the contemplating mind the transparent sign of the end. The organic is, in Schleiermacher's expression, at the same time the symbolic, in so far as the forming thought can be recognised in its product. To him who looks more deeply organic form betrays the secret of the creating mind. The expression of the form is the starting-point of thought that seeks the end, and again the goal of thought that projects and carries out the end it has found. In this sense one may say that the forms of phenomena are the written characters of God.

[[469]] 15. In so far as the true is a real category (as a modal one it can become clear only later), it fits in here. For there has formed, for truth, which we otherwise place in the subjective agreement of our representation with its object, an objective sense, in that we call the real whole that, belonging to a genus, corresponds through and through to the inner end, that is, the individual that is representative of a genus, a true individual; as we call, for example, Socrates a true philosopher, Pericles a true statesman, and further down, among plants and animals, the specimen that presents the genus purely and fully, the true plant, the true animal of such a genus; or as we call the state that corresponds to its end in all the functions belonging to it and fulfils its ethical sense a true state. Where we remain within the efficient cause, we use, analogously, the actual; yet we can transfer the true thither as well, namely when for the individual the law of its essence is thought as a task, as when we speak of a true circle if ⟦160⟧ the task was to draw one. In this great objective sense Plato takes truth when he ascribes it to the idea of the Good and says of it in the Philebus that whoever would determine its difficult concept necessarily comes upon beauty, symmetry and truth.¹

[[470]] 16. The concept of the beautiful too springs from this soil, in that the inner end has taken up into itself an essential relation to the one who intuits.

[[471]] Language, forming its designations by the tact of need and moving in the analogy of the association of ideas, is more liberal with the expression of the beautiful than the philosophical aesthetician. Where a sensuous intuition is agreeable, it calls it beautiful. Agreement with the apprehending organ is here its measure, and we even hear talk of a beautiful smell and a beautiful taste. Language then pursues the agreement that produces pleasure in the one who intuits from the sensuous into the mental, and attributes beauty even to truth when it makes itself known to the inquirer in its harmony; indeed the poet attributes to it a beauty such as no painter and no sculptor could represent.²

[[472]] In connection with what precedes there results the narrower concept of organic beauty.

[[473]] When form not merely satisfies the individual real end but, destined for intuition, at the same time corresponds to the ideal ends of intuition, so that understanding and imagination, as Kant puts it, are set into a harmonious play, articulated form becomes organic beauty. What determines remains in this the end. The beautiful form of the male body is apprehended first of all according to the representation of the male essence. This inner end is what rules. When it lends the form such ⟦161⟧ expression that the form, which is to step into appearance, also corresponds to the ends of appearance by harmoniously stimulating intuition, the organ of appearance, then this fusion of the inner and outer end is what is peculiar to organic beauty. Since its symmetry seems to be produced only by its own end, because this end, present in all parts of the form, shows through everywhere, it seems in turn to be there only for intuition, which becomes conscious in it of its own harmony. Thus objective consideration and subjective contemplation agree in wondrous satisfaction; and in this even stimulation of concept and sense lies the charm of intuition.¹

[[476]] 17. Here a view opens into the ethical categories. All moral concepts rest on the end. To be sure, elements are added that go beyond the end alone — knowledge and free disposition. In the moral sphere man becomes the judging free organ of his inner destination, or, as we rightly feel, since his destination does not derive from him, the judging free organ of a divine end. The categories of the end are therefore heightened in the ethical and determine themselves in a peculiar way.

[[477]] Even in the concept of the person we think, as fundamental concept, a whole that gathers itself together (substance), but self-conscious and willing in its ends and its causality. In man the organism is its presupposition. Although the I that is a person distinguishes itself from its body, it nevertheless comprehends itself together with it. The ethical community in turn comprehends itself as a moral organism and therein becomes, as for example in the state, a person at a higher stage. Further, what accords with or contradicts the inner end of human nature ⟦162⟧ becomes good or evil through the character of disposition and freedom. Knowledge of the end in its whole relation becomes wisdom, the devoted deed of it becomes love, living personal measure becomes temperance (Plato's σωφροσύνη), the intensity of the instrument for the end perseverance, the relation of the member to the whole (inherence) obedience, the reciprocal action of the members within a whole justice (in the Platonic sense). To organic beauty there is added, at the deepest ground, the harmony of knowing and willing, the accord of inner freedom, and from this comes forth, with a more beautiful countenance than any other, moral beauty.

[[480]] The concepts which, peculiar to ethics, raise the categories of the end to a higher stage — in particular the knowing free personality — cannot be investigated here. It was only a matter of indicating in a few outlines how the moral concepts grow out of the universal element of the categories. A working out and a more exact determination is not in place here, and only the continuous development was to be designated. The divine end, which possesses bound organs in nature and free organs in man, links the realm of nature and of freedom and is the living middle concept of two worlds otherwise separated.

[[481]] 18. In what precedes, the good — which, even when used shallowly, is always related to an end, if only an external one, and has no sense prior to the end — has been designated in its ethical meaning. Metaphysics since Plato has raised the concept to the idea of the Good and set it equal to the perfect; and in this sense the concept holds together the true, the good and the beautiful, each of which has fused within itself and harmoniously attuned a great opposition, namely inner destination and actuality, willing and knowing, the appearance of ends and intuition, ⟦163⟧ and unites them into a new harmony. The ideas of the true, the good and the beautiful, so often set side by side as if separate, but all going back to the inner end, rather demand one another for unity, and each is impoverished without the others.

[[482]] 19. In this way the categories developed from motion take the end up into themselves and become more determinate. What is still abstract in them points to intuition. Motion, the first principle, produced intuition, and the end, the second, presupposed it. Hence the derived fundamental concepts are capable of developing in the most manifold shape and of individualising themselves out of experience in progressive law. Their own act underlies them as creative principle, and in this rests their clarity, in this for us the possibility of casting a full glance into their mental birth. The same act reveals itself in the world, and in this rests the fullness of their application and the possibility of comprehending phenomena through them and of enriching them in turn through phenomena. Thus the infinite world of concepts comes to be and grows on a simple ground.

[[483]] It is the old sense of the doctrine of categories to find the fundamental concepts which, in the motley, intertwining web of our representations, give hold and light to all the others; and it has been the new task since Kant to distinguish in them the origin from the mind or from experience, the a priori or the a posteriori. Both have been attempted in the sketch of the real categories and of their new stamping by the concept of end. Symmetry, such as for example the ordering by triads, which attracts the mind bent on survey in a subjective interest and which it therefore readily takes to be original and objective, has not been striven for here, because from the fundamental activity many sides proceed, as at *one* stroke, which, grasped in concepts, become categories. It is foolish to disdain the opened source of the real fundamental concepts ⟦164⟧ on the ground that what it yields does not fit into a preconceived schema that has only psychological value. The survey of the categories does not need symmetry as a prop; it is in and for itself clear enough when from motion space and time, figure and number proceed, and thereby the quantum together with measure becomes possible; when motion as efficient cause, presenting itself within itself as reciprocal action, makes intelligible the projection of form and space-filling matter; when in the difference of its productive activity it founds substances through the law of formation and causally produces in them the concept of quality. We prefer insight into a genesis that has become intelligible to the architectonic charm of symmetry and the pleasing overview. How have the concepts not been stretched and forcibly split in order to obey the preconceived symmetrical law! In the kindred eight grammatical parts of speech there is likewise no such façade of concepts, and we do not miss it. On the other hand there has resulted for us, in connection with the principles of the sciences, chiefly through the determining end, a gradation of the fundamental concepts which, running through all of them, has a symmetrical effect of itself and not seldom brings into the homonymy of words deeper differences, as it were kept silent by language. The same fundamental concepts, projected spontaneously in the mathematical, are filled in the physical, deepened in the organic, exalted in the ethical. Thus we saw the same fundamental concept of the whole closed in itself, which in figure and number is substance only in the abstract sense, become material and concrete in nature, rise through the end to the organism in the living, and be transfigured in the ethical in the person and in the moral organism. Thus we saw further the concept of the part, originally arising with the mathematical quantum, become concrete in nature, become member through the end in the organic, and through the end in ⟦165⟧ the human destination return more freely as member of the ethical community. Thus we saw likewise the concept of measure, springing from external comparison in mathematical activity, determined more inwardly in the physical domain, rising to ideal measure and symmetry in the organic, and in the ethical even becoming free in a personal virtue. Thus we saw further the fundamental concept of properties, a causality adhering to substance, run through the same stages, becoming in the organic the principle of reflexive activities and in the ethical even virtues. This gradation shows us already in the fundamental concepts the real significance of that methodical progress which determines the universal through the specific difference. The stages present themselves in such a way that the mathematical and physical on the one side and the organic and ethical on the other appear in close kinship, because on the former only the efficient cause with its whence, on the latter the end with its whither, is the determining power. The end does not thereby break in like a fate (it has been reproached with this), nor does it come blindly upon the blindly working cause; for it is itself providence. The end is the higher concept for the sake of which the lower exists, not the reverse; and whoever could place himself at once at the highest standpoint would be able to find the categories backwards from the directing end. We have said above why we did not take this path. Seen from the absolute, if we may anticipate so high, the end is what is original and everything else what follows for it; but seen from our side, motion (the efficient cause) is what is nearest, better known, simpler. Here we must rate highly an advantage of this path which we already brought out when we inferred the sketch of the categories from motion. Even the most developed categories are connected by their foundation with intuition and have in it evidence and applicability.

[[484]] ⟦166⟧ These real categories determined from the end are formed by the mind necessarily; and even materialism, which would disenchant the world of everything ideal, strives in vain to be rid of them. For as long as man knows consciously or feels unconsciously that in the inner end — which, even if it could derive from its opposite, chance, would remain the reason of things — he possesses the guiding thought without which he can preserve nothing, heal nothing, perfect nothing; as long as he therefore projects, according to the inner end, measures or ideals by which he judges life or strives to complete it: so long will these fundamental concepts unfailingly spring up in him.

[[485]] In the world of values — we borrow this expression — these fundamental concepts will always be produced and produced again; for without the end there is no value, and without the inner end of humanity no dignity.
