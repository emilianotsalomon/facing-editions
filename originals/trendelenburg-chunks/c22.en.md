[[1597]] # ⟦530⟧ XXIV. RETROSPECT.

[[1598]] Each of the above investigations, to be sure, strives for a decided result, and each that follows takes this up from the one preceding it as an acquired possession, in order to gain something new beyond it; and to that extent the sections close of themselves into a circle. Since, however, when occupied with the parts, one all too easily loses sight of the whole, we shall attempt to gather the individual views into *one* glance, and we recall the connection in a few fleeting outlines.¹

[[1599]] All sciences carry within them metaphysical presuppositions in their object and logical presuppositions in their method; all are intent upon producing necessity, in which object and method, being and thinking, metaphysical and logical elements are peculiarly united. The question of what right the presuppositions have, and how such a unification comes about, demands a theory of science, which may be called logic in the wider sense.

[[1600]] Formal logic accomplishes something essential for method, but it does not satisfy the task designated. Hegel's ⟦531⟧ dialectic, on the other hand, promises more, indeed the greatest that can be thought, but it is impossible.

[[1602]] Can that, then, be impossible which has long been at work and still is at work, and which is therefore surely actual? We have no other answer to this question than the investigations themselves. Moreover Goethe, an often cited authority, says: "Whereas the observer, the natural scientist, torments himself with what is false, because the phenomena at every moment contradict the opinion, the philosopher can still go on operating in his sphere with a false result, since no result is so false that it could not in some way hold good as form without any content." Philosophy will escape this misfortune only if, like the other sciences, it strives out of thinking into intuition, and measures the thought by the intuition and the intuition by the thought.

[[1603]] For us human beings there is no pure thinking; for, like a soul without a body, it would have no life without intuition, but only a ghostly, spectral existence. Thinking kills itself when it renounces the world of intuition. In vain does it hope thereby to become divine thinking and to present this in its eternity, as it was before the creation of things. The divine thinking thought the world and had therein an intuition. Human thinking only re-creates this thought which has become bodily. Hence the first principle of thinking must be one that leads into intuition and produces its possibility. Without such a principle there is no community between thinking and things.

[[1604]] It was in this significance that motion appeared, the word taken not metaphorically but in a sensible meaning. In the mind it projects figures and numbers and produces the possibility of the great a priori science which we admire in pure mathematics. In matter motion embodies itself in fixed forms, and since it is common to the mind and ⟦532⟧ to things, it grounds the possibility of applying the pure mathematical element in experience. Thus motion, as an activity identical for mind and nature, is the key to the greatest and most extensive products of human knowledge.

[[1605]] The same original activity, constructive motion, is the efficient ground when the mind appropriates the external world through the senses. While it receives from without, it is active from within through projecting motion. This mental share in sense perception appears, upon closer investigation, in the very midst of the empirical, and is clearly to be recognised especially in the higher senses. Thus motion is the a priori condition of sensible knowledge.

[[1606]] Matter is, in this domain, the given substrate. So far as the mind understands it, it understands it only through motion, which extends it and holds it together. Only through motion does it comprehend matter as filling space. But something uncomprehended remains behind, in which a unity of being and activity must be assumed.

[[1607]] In matter motion is causal; it posits substances in determinate shape, produces properties in them, gives them magnitude and measure, and embraces them with the unity which binds the parts in reciprocal action. Here it creates outwardly, and in things themselves, the categories, which arise from it, as the original mental act, equally in the mind, and form the necessary order of the mind's view of the world. The universal origin of the categories, the possibility of their more determinate development, and their warrant, as much real as logical, lie in motion as an act creative in mind and in matter. From the living and consistent development of this act proceed the fundamental lines of our physical view of the world.

[[1608]] The critical investigation of pure thinking pointed, in its result, toward seeking a principle which, as a fundamental activity of thinking, would lead into intuition ⟦533⟧ (I. p. 130). If some have wished to find a contradiction in this task, because thinking is not intuition and intuition is not thinking, this contradiction springs for the most part from the word, and consideration of the matter has resolved it. We took thinking in the wider sense, where, as in the sketch of the universal problem (I. p. 130 ff.), we set thinking, as the subjective activity of appropriation in general, over against being as the object. In this wider meaning intuition belongs to thinking. Where, on the other hand, the Kantian conception opposes intuition and thinking, like sensibility and understanding, as mutually exclusive, and separates the categories, as root concepts of the understanding, from the principles of intuition, there it restricts thinking in a way that is untenable (cf. above I. p. 387 f.). Between constructive motion and the categories, which go back into it, there exists, as has been shown, no opposition. If, finally, linguistic usage conceives thinking in the most compressed sense, such that its essence is to find the ground of things, then there does indeed lie, in the ordinary representation, a gulf between the principle of intuition and the ground. In the view set out here, however, no such gulf exists at all. For the same constructive motion which mediates intuition discloses causality, since the causes of things are modes of motion. So little is there a contradiction here, whether one takes thinking in that meaning which comprises intuition or in this narrow one which sets itself against intuition. Rather, what otherwise remains unmediated goes back into this unity of origin.

[[1609]] Now since such a community exists between thinking and being, not only can things determine thought, so that it reproduces them mentally in the concept, but thought can also determine things, so that they present it bodily. Where it is already realised, it finds itself again. There the thought is prior to the appearance, and the parts stem ⟦534⟧ from the prefigured whole, not, as otherwise, the whole from the parts. The mind knows the end, since it itself projects ends. Here once again a power presents itself which belongs in common to thinking and being. The inner end now becomes principle, motion only foundation.

[[1610]] The end fuses with motion; for since it directs motion, it is itself motion. While the efficient cause is intuited as the whence, the end appears as the whither.

[[1611]] The end, as inner end, becomes the individuating principle of beings, and the soul is a self-realising thought of an end.

[[1612]] The end determines the real categories that sprang from spatial motion, by stamping itself upon them. Thereby they receive an ideal and mental significance, and since the end is the fundamental concept of the practical sphere, these categories reach into the ethical, in which the inner destination is known and willed. The end, in its world-governing significance, forms the fundamental lines of our organic view of the world, according to which the mind is the formative soul of things and things are the instrument of the mind. In it the reciprocal action of thinking and being is completed. But here arises the struggle of the sciences among one another and the conflict of theories within one and the same science; the one maintains the efficient cause alone, the other seeks to subordinate it to the end. Only the matter itself can decide; yet however the decision may fall in the particular case, there always remains the belief in the mental harmony of the whole, in which the discord may after all resolve itself into the unity of the mind.

[[1613]] Motion and end are the activities identical for thinking and being. The mind would have to deny itself if it wished to give them up. Rather, as it develops them in things, the necessary results for it. If this is defined as that which could not be otherwise, ⟦535⟧ then the definition points to originally fixed points, from which the attempt to see whether something could be otherwise is repulsed. These must be common to thinking and being, since otherwise they could never hold for both, never be applicable to both. The necessary is therefore, like the possible, a double formation, in which logical and real elements meet or interpenetrate one another.

[[1614]] What is to serve for such a development, by being equally original to thinking and being, can be no resting point, no fixed form. Under such a form one can indeed subsume other things; but the relation remains external, and the right of subsumption presupposes a higher, comprehensive activity from which it itself stems. Hence, in particular, neither could space and time be ready-made forms of intuition, nor the categories ready-made root concepts of the understanding. Rather, both well forth from developing motion and its products. This recognition of the original activity is difficult from many sides, but extremely important. For "the worst that can befall science is that one takes the derived for the original, and, since one cannot derive the original from the derived, seeks to explain the original from the derived. From this arises an endless confusion, a lumber of words, and a continual effort to seek and find evasions wherever the true merely begins to come forth and seeks to become powerful."

[[1615]] In this whole course one will not fail to recognise the method of a hypothesis that proves itself in its consequences. Motion, constructive in the mind, corresponding to spatial motion, was hypothetically adopted as elementary mediation (I. p. 140), and proved itself as such in its consequences step by step in the investigation of the stages of knowledge (cf. II. p. 447 ff.). Since the a priori has the destination of making experience possible, the proof of constructive motion had to lie in its being found again as a ⟦536⟧ condition of empirical knowledge. This demand too was fulfilled in the investigation of sensible knowledge (I. p. 238 ff.), as well as in the conception of matter (I. p. 266 f.). In this inner agreement confidence in the correctness of the beginning grows.

[[1616]] The community of thinking and being here set forth shows itself further in this, that the forms of thinking correspond to the forms of being, even though they differ essentially in that the former are universal, the latter individual. As in being substance proceeds from activity and activities in turn from substance, so concepts arise from judgments, and judgments from concepts. The relation of ground and consequence in thinking corresponds, in being, to the relation of cause and effect. Since already in the judgment the producing activity of the thing is what determines, grounding is, as it were, only an expanded judgment. The necessity of consequence flows from the points in which thinking and being meet; for as a thing comes to be, so only is it understood in the ultimate sense. The development of a principle yields the system of a science in the same way as a real domain is governed by a law.

[[1617]] The unconditioned, to which the systems of the finite sciences point, goes beyond the concepts which hold for the conditioned mind and conditioned things. It cannot be said how far these finite categories adequately express the essence and life of the infinite. Yet what is necessary in the conditioned cannot be contingent in the unconditioned. By an indirect path the necessity confronts the mind of positing the absolute, and indeed of positing it in such a way that the unity of the view of the world becomes, as it were, the bodily counter-image, visible to us, of the creative mind. Hence we must grasp the world in its depth in order to understand God in his essence. To this all sciences must contribute, so that there may form an organic view of the world grounded in firm particulars, in which nothing actual is ⟦537⟧ without thought and no thought without realisation, in which things present the actuality of the divine idea and the divine idea is the truth of things. In such a view the world is the glory of God and God the presupposition of the world. Where the individual sciences work in hostilely opposed directions, there philosophy has the task of reconciling them in the thought of the whole born of mind, and of leading them toward the presentation of the *one* organic view of the world.

[[1618]] In the organic consideration of things there shows itself everywhere the unity of an opposition which is the image of the opposition of soul and body. The one factor is the higher and governing, the other the external and presenting. Thus in the word the mental representation and the sensible sound have become one; thus in organic motion we distinguish the activity of the instruments that change place and the directing glance; in all the senses the external impression and the inner reproduction. The same organic difference and organic unity is found again in the logical, and reveals itself, peculiarly shaped, in the individual spheres.

[[1619]] Motion becomes the organ of the end. As motion constitutes the essence of matter, so the end inspirits motion. Concept and intuition correspond to each other and interpenetrate one another, and ground and appearance, unity and multiplicity, content and extension, the idea of the whole and the actuality of the parts, the universal forms of thinking and the forms of being bound in the individual, all reveal in their way the same opposition and the same unity. If, according to a beautiful saying, thinking is the longing out of limitation into infinity, then conversely it is just as much the plastic drive out of the infinite into determinate shape.

[[1620]] "As the eye is offered the dark, so it demands the light; it demands dark when one brings light ⟦538⟧ before it, and precisely thereby shows its liveliness, its right to grasp the object, by producing out of itself something that is opposed to the object." So the mind produces the concept for the intuition and the intuition for the concept, and reveals its creative power in free mastery over the greatest opposition of the world.

[[1621]] As the eye is harmoniously stimulated by the oppositions of colours, since through them it becomes conscious of its whole living force, so the mind too is satisfied only when it brings forth, in the symmetry of concept and intuition, the full expression of its whole essence.

[[1622]] To this satisfaction of the knowing mind the truth of things corresponds. When the end, as foreseeing thought and directing will, becomes the origin of the otherwise blind motion, there presents itself a subordination of the real to the ideal, a realisation of the ideal in the real. The philosophy which grounds and carries this through renounces the ambiguous identity of the subjective and objective, but it unites realism and idealism.
